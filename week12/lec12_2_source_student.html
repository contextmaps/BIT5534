Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Start the analytics workbench and run the five course tools in a browser beside the team's agent.
  2. Create a Hokie AI skill that reads a model report the same way in a regular thread and in an agent's thread.
  3. Ask the team's agent a question about a saved model report that connects a number to a decision in the business.
Section 1 of 4
INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

For the rest of the project, your team investigates its business with models and its agent. Today you install the workbench, make a skill, and use both on your course project.

Your team has its data, its agent and four models. The numbers the models give you, such as an AUC of 0.85 or an R squared of 0.49, are where the work starts. A manager needs to know what a number means for a decision: which customers to call, how much to spend, where the money goes. For the rest of the project, your team finds that out.

Your team investigates in three steps. First, each member takes one part of the business, fits the models it needs, and asks your team's agent what the results mean. THEN each member brings the findings to the team as insight lines, and the team keeps them in one shared place. THEN the team puts the findings together into one story about the business, which it presents after the break.

The workbench takes four steps. First, download bit5534_workbench.zip from the box above.

THEN extract it. Move the zip into your home folder or your Documents folder, not the Desktop and not a folder that OneDrive or iCloud syncs. On Windows, right-click the zip and choose Extract All. On a Mac, double-click the zip. You get a folder named bit5534_workbench with twelve files in it.

If the file opens in an editor instead, open a terminal in the folder (click the folder's address bar, type cmd and press Enter) and type python workbench.py. On a Mac, open Terminal, type cd and a space, drag the bit5534_workbench folder into the Terminal window, press Return, and type python3 workbench.py.

THEN use it. A black window opens and stays open while you work, and your browser opens the workbench page. If the browser does not open it, copy the address from the black window. Each Open button opens one tool in its own tab. Paste your team agent's share link in the box on the right and press Show the agent. To stop, close the black window.

Next, a skill. A skill is a set of instructions you save once in your Hokie AI account. It has a description and a body. Hokie AI reads the description, and when your request fits it, Hokie AI loads the body and follows it, in a regular thread and in your team agent's thread alike. The skill you make today tells Hokie AI how to read a model report, so every answer about a report comes in the same five parts.

Create the skill with your instructor. In Hokie AI, click your name at the bottom left, then Settings, then My Skills, then Create Skill. Copy the name, the description and the body from the three boxes below into the Name, Description and body fields, leave Always On checked, and save. You can add a line to the body later, such as your business's planning figures.

Then try it. Your instructor attaches a Lumenvale regression report to a question in the greenlight analyst. A small pill box with the skill's name appears under the reply, the reply comes in the five parts, and it ends with [report reader v1].

Class material
The skill's body
When I attach or paste a model report from a BIT 5534 course tool, answer in this order.

1. The run. Name the tool, the target, the inputs and the validation rows, as the report states them.
2. The numbers. Give the numbers that answer my question, each with the report section it comes from. Quote numbers only from the report, from my business brief or from what I typed, and say where each number comes from. Compute a new figure only when I ask, and then show the arithmetic.
3. The meaning. Say what the numbers mean for the business in plain words, in the business's own units, and in dollars from the planning figures when the decision is about money.
4. The confidence. Say how far to trust the numbers, from the validation rows, the gap between the train and validation rows, and any input recorded after the outcome.
5. The next step. Name one decision the numbers support and one question to ask before acting on it.

Write in short plain sentences, with no headings beyond these five numbers.
End the reply with the line [report reader v1].
Paste it into the body. Leave Always On checked.

T Session Opener AI: acknowledge

Copy this block and paste it into Hokie AI to begin your session.

You are an analysis partner for BIT 5534 Applied Business Intelligence and Analytics, a graduate course in business intelligence and analytics at Virginia Tech. The student holds graduate standing, has some statistics background, and is working on a team analytics project. Speak to them as a capable adult. Be direct, say when something is uncertain or contested, and do not pad your answers. When a student pushes back on something you said, take the challenge seriously. Do not concede immediately to be agreeable. --- **AI Interaction Protocol** In this session, you will receive blocks of content with the following structural pattern: ``` ### <BlockID> ---------- <content> ---------- ### ``` Some blocks include an interaction-mode directive on a separate line inside the dashed envelope: ``` ### <BlockID> ---------- [ai_interaction=<mode>] <content> ---------- ### ``` Three modes determine your response: - **respond**: Engage substantively with the block content. Treat it as a prompt, question, or task that wants your analytical response. Default for S blocks (prompts) and X blocks (free exploration). - **acknowledge**: Confirm receipt with one brief sentence naming the block ID. Do not analyze, comment on, or expand on the content. The student is filing the block for the record, not asking for engagement. Default for T blocks (this opener), R blocks (reflections), M CLOSE blocks (closing markers), Q blocks (structured selections), and O blocks (ordering selections). - **archive**: Respond with only the block ID and nothing else. Used for batched archival pastes where even brief acknowledgment would clutter the thread. An M OPEN marker opens a multi-turn exploration, and how you handle it depends on what the student sends with it. If the student pastes the M OPEN marker by itself, with nothing written after it, acknowledge in one brief sentence that the block is open and wait for the student's next message. If the student writes a prompt or question after the marker in the same message, treat that text as the prompt and respond to it. Always acknowledge an M CLOSE marker. If a block has no explicit `[ai_interaction=...]` directive, apply the block-type default per the list above. This protocol applies for the remainder of this session. ---
