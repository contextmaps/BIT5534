Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Run the routing tool they built in lecture 2.2 on a dataset it has not seen, and report which branch it took and what sent it there.
  2. Say what a test for unequal variance reports about the model that was fitted, and why the finding is not a statement about how carefully anything was measured.
  3. Say what a two way ANOVA adds to a one way ANOVA, and read an interaction off a table of cell means.
  4. Say what would count as evidence that two factors interact, and what would count as evidence that they do not.
  5. Specify a tool that answers whether two factors interact, and say which part of its output carries the evidence.
  6. Write a specification for a tool in their own words and close its gaps through an interview they asked for and bounded.
  7. Say which of their case one conclusions survived the arrival of a second factor and which did not.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 60 points, 15 for the first M block, 30 for the second M block and 15 for the reflection in the R block.

The file below is the same study with one more column. Every row now records who the person was as well as which drug they took.

Case one fitted one grouping variable, which was the drug. In ANOVA a grouping variable is called a factor, and each of its categories, drug A, drug B and drug C, is called a level. A two way ANOVA fits two factors at once and asks something a one way ANOVA cannot, which is whether the differences among the levels of one factor change across the levels of the other. You will learn that pattern in the first M block and build a tool for it in the second. Work in the same thread.

Class material
The data for case two
Download pain_relief.csv
The same three hundred and thirty rows, with a gender column between the pain score and the drug. Save it beside the other file.

Open the first M block and learn the two way ANOVA before you specify anything. Ten turns is the ceiling here as well, and no script comes out of this one.

Use as many turns as you need inside the ten and keep the conversation coherent, so that each question follows from the answer before it. Four answered well beats ten that stay on the surface. Come out of it able to say four things in your own words.

What a two way ANOVA does that a one way ANOVA cannot. What a table of cell means is, and what its six numbers show that three group means do not. What an interaction between two factors is, said as a pattern in that table. What would count as evidence that two factors interact, and what would count as evidence that they do not.

Cell means and interaction are both new to this course. Ask what each one is before you ask how to test for one, because a test means nothing until you know what it is testing.

Push on the last one. Knowing what an interaction looks like is only half of it, because you cannot recognise a pattern without knowing what its absence looks like. Ask what the table of cell means and the picture look like when the two factors do not interact at all, so you know what you are looking for before you build anything.

Before you go on, save your case one output or leave it open in another window. The case two reflection asks you to set your case two result beside the three drug means from case one, and you will need them in front of you.
