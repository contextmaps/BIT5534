Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Run a regression tool they built through conversation on a dataset it has not seen, and verify it against known values before reading any coefficient.
  2. Read a variance inflation factor and a p-value on the same row of an output table and say what each one reports about that coefficient.
  3. Separate the predictors whose coefficients this sample can tell apart from zero from the ones it cannot, and say what the model as a whole still establishes.
  4. Say what stepwise regression is and what it produces that one fit of all the predictors does not.
  5. Say what AIC and BIC each measure, and how the two differ in how many predictors they keep.
  6. Write a specification for a tool in their own words and close its gaps through an interview they asked for.
  7. Obtain a stepwise regression tool, run it, and compare the subsets it produces against each other and against the case one table.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 60 points, 15 for the first M block, 30 for the second M block and 15 for the reflection in the R block.

Case one gave you one regression with all ten predictors in it and no rule for deciding which of them to keep. Stepwise regression is one answer, and it is what case two is about. It fits a sequence of regressions, adding or removing one predictor at a time, and it uses a number called an information criterion to decide when to stop. What it hands back is a subset, which is a shorter list of predictors than the ten you started with.

You will learn it in the first M block and build it in the second. Work in the same thread.

Open the first M block and learn stepwise regression before you specify anything.

Ten turns is the ceiling here as well, and no script comes out of this one. Use as many as you need and keep the conversation coherent, so that each question follows from the answer before it. Four answered well beats ten that stay on the surface. Come out of it able to say four things in your own words.

What stepwise regression produces that the single fit in case one did not. What AIC and BIC each measure, how each one penalises a model for carrying an extra predictor, and whether a low value or a high value is the better one. How the two penalties differ, and which of the two criteria tends to keep more predictors. What the forward, backward and adaptive searches each do, whether the three of them have to stop at the same subset, and when two criterion values can be set against each other.

AIC and BIC are new to this course. Ask what each one is before you ask how they differ, because the difference will not mean anything until you know what each is measuring.

Forward and backward each have an obvious place to begin. Adaptive does not, so ask whether the word names one procedure or several. If it names several, find out which choices separate them, including where the search starts, and settle those choices yourself. A default you did not choose will decide part of your answer.

Before you go on, save your case one output or leave it open in another window. The case two reflection asks you to put the predictors that were significant in that table beside the subsets stepwise regression chooses, and you will need the table in front of you to do it.
