Learning Objectives

By the end of this progress report, each team will be able to:

  1. Present the course project, from the business brief to a recommendation, in at most 15 slides and 12 minutes.
  2. Support each finding with a number, the model and report from which it was obtained, and its validation.
  3. Show how the team worked with its agent and how the recommendation with the greatest consequences would be governed.
  4. Deliver the presentation as a team, with every member speaking.

ORIENTATION

Progress report 3 is the final deliverable of the course project. Each team presents to the class what it learned about its business. The presentation is built from progress reports 1 and 2, in particular from the decisions, the big picture, the governance box and the story in the progress report 2 team thread.

Progress report 3 consists of two Canvas assignments with the same deadline. One member uploads the team's slides, worth 100 points, to Progress Report 3 from this page. After each presentation, every student evaluates the presenting team on the five criteria described on this page. At the end of each presentation session, every student also ranks the teams that presented in that session. The instructor sets each team's grade from the evaluations and the rankings. Each member's share of the team grade is then adjusted according to the peer evaluations, as in progress reports 1 and 2.

Section 2 of 5
THE STORY

The presentation is organized as the story of the investigation, in the order in which the team carried it out.

  1. The business. Present the decisions the business faces and their financial impact, using the planning figures in the brief.
  2. The exploration. Explain how the work was divided, which models were fitted, how the text explorer was used if the team used it, and which findings mattered most. These are taken from the insight lines and the decisions in the team thread.
  3. The agent. Show the team's conversation with its agent about the findings, including the live data the agent retrieved.
  4. Governance. For the recommendation with the greatest consequences, state its main risk, the safeguard, who decides, what is monitored and what would stop the action. These are taken from the governance box.
  5. Business intelligence. State what the findings mean for the business, what the manager should do first and its expected value, and which questions remain open. These are taken from the big picture and the story.

A presentation is stronger with two further elements. The first is an assumption in the brief that was contradicted by the data, shown with the number and the report from which it was obtained. The second is a result the team set aside because it appeared better than it was, for example a model fitted with a column recorded after the outcome, or an accuracy equal to the share of the most common answer. The team should explain why the result was set aside.

The following rules apply to every team.

  1. The presentation has at most 15 slides, including the title slide and the Questions slide.
  2. The presentation lasts at most 12 minutes. The instructor keeps time and stops the presentation at 12 minutes.
  3. Each slide contains little text, in type of 20 points or larger and about 30 words or fewer. The rest is said aloud.
  4. All slides use one design, with the same fonts, colors and layout.
  5. The agent is shown in screenshots, with one exchange and one live data call. The presentations run on the instructor's computer, so a live demonstration is not possible.
  6. Every member presents. The slides are divided fairly among the members, and the presentation is rehearsed individually and as a team, with a timer.
  7. Presenters wear business casual dress.
  8. The presentation is saved as a PowerPoint file in 16:9 format, with fonts embedded and without video or links that require a login. The file is then compressed into a .zip file named with the team code, such as PM06_PR3.zip.

THE EVALUATION

Each criterion is stated as a sentence about the strongest presentation and is worth 20 points. After each presentation, every student rates agreement with each sentence on a five-point scale. The instructor reviews the ratings and the rankings to set the team grade. Teams should read the five sentences before building their slides.

The five criteria
The five criteria, 20 points each

1. The business problem
   At the beginning of the presentation, the team stated the decisions the business
   faces and their financial impact, using the figures in the brief.

2. The evidence
   The team supported each key finding with a number, the model and report from which
   the number was obtained, and its validation.

3. The agent and governance
   The team showed an exchange with its agent and a live data call, and explained how
   the recommendation with the greatest consequences would be governed.

4. The business intelligence
   The team based its recommendation on its findings and stated what the manager should
   do first and the expected value of that action.

5. The delivery
   The team finished within 12 minutes, used clear and consistent slides, and every
   member presented.

After each presentation, every student rates agreement with each statement on a five-
