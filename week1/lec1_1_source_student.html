Welcome to BIT 5534. Today sets up the way we will work for the rest of the semester, and introduces one habit that matters more than any single technique in the course.

An AI assistant will answer almost anything you ask, immediately, in confident prose. The first answer is seldom the useful one. The useful answer arrives after you have pushed, after you have asked for the case where the idea breaks, or asked it to justify a claim, or noticed that it skipped something. The course is built around the second answer.

Everything you do today happens in one conversation, and the conversation is what you submit.

S1.1.1 Standard Prompt AI: respond
I need to install Python on my own computer so that I can write and run small data analysis scripts for a graduate business analytics course. Assume I have not installed a programming language before. Start by asking me what operating system I am on and whether anything is already installed. Then give me the steps one at a time and wait for me to confirm each one before you give me the next. Use the official installer from python.org. Do not suggest an approach that requires me to set up a package manager first. When the installation is done, give me one command I can run to check that it worked, tell me exactly what a correct result looks like, and tell me what to do if I see something different.

The next block is a multi-turn block, an M block. The installation prompt was a single prompt, an S block. You will meet both forms often. An S block carries one instruction. An M block holds a whole exchange and is read as a unit.

Now a question about statistics. Variance is the average of the squared deviations from the mean. Nearly everyone who has taken a statistics course has computed one. Far fewer can say why the deviations are squared.

Two ways of summarizing how far the observations sit from the mean are written below. Both are written for a population. The sample versions divide by n − 1 and replace μ with the sample mean. That difference has an answer of its own, and it is not the question we are asking today.

Ask HokieAI why variance squares the deviations instead of taking absolute values. The first answer will probably be that squaring prevents positive and negative deviations from cancelling. The statement is true and incomplete, because an absolute value prevents the same cancellation. So push. Ask what squaring accomplishes that an absolute value does not. Ask what it costs. Ask which of the two reacts more strongly to a single unusual observation, and whether that reaction is useful. Ask for a case where the choice would change a business decision.

Take at least three turns after the first answer. Use one question from the list to begin. Then respond to claims HokieAI actually makes. At least one of your later questions should be a follow up to an answer you received, which can be a point you doubt, or a consequence you want to test. Follow the exchange where it leads.
