SHARE 1, THE PAYMENT PLAN OFFER (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 The bill jump
Add a column: bill_jump, equal to usage_kwh divided by usual_monthly_kwh. Fit the logistic regression of unpaid_60 in reg_explorer.py with it.
Ask: "Does a month's usage far above the household's usual usage raise the chance of an unpaid bill, with the other inputs held fixed? How does that compare with the size of the bill itself?"
Look for: the estimate for bill_jump against the estimate for usage_kwh; whether the validation AUC rose over the model without it.

1.2 Who gets an offer
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on unpaid_60, with bill_jump among the inputs. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "An offer costs about 8 dollars and prevents about a third of the nonpayment it targets. An unpaid bill costs about 40 percent of the bill plus 60 dollars of staff time. For a 250 dollar bill, at what chance of nonpayment does an offer pay? How many Validation households are above it?"
Look for: the break even chance with the arithmetic shown; the counts at that cutoff.

1.3 LIVE DATA: the coming week
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve the coming week's forecast, and to say which households should receive an offer before the bills go out, from your findings in 1.1 and 4.1.

SHARE 2, THE BUDGET BILLING PUSH (decision 2)

2.1 Budget billing and nonpayment
Fit and save: the logistic regression of unpaid_60.
Ask: "How much lower is the chance of an unpaid bill for households on budget billing, with the other inputs held fixed?"
Look for: the estimate for budget_billing; the limit in your brief that households who choose it may already plan carefully.

2.2 Does budget billing remove the jump
Add a column: jump_budget, equal to bill_jump when budget_billing is Yes and 0 otherwise. Refit the logistic regression of unpaid_60.
Ask: "Does a bill jump matter less for households on budget billing? Which households should be invited before winter?"
Look for: the estimate for jump_budget; a rule for whom to invite.

2.3 Explore: the value of an invitation
Ask the agent what inviting the riskiest thousand households to budget billing would save in a winter, and which number in its answer it trusts least.

SHARE 3, THE POWER PURCHASE (decision 3)

3.1 Temperature and usage
Fit and save: the regression and the tree of usage_kwh.
Ask: "How does usage change with avg_temp_f? Does it fall steadily as the month gets warmer, or does it turn and rise again? Use the splits in the tree."
Look for: the regression slope against the tree's splits; the temperature where usage is lowest.

3.2 Heating and cooling
Add two columns: cold_degrees, equal to the larger of 0 and 65 minus avg_temp_f, and warm_degrees, equal to the larger of 0 and avg_temp_f minus 65. Refit the regression of usage_kwh with them in place of avg_temp_f.
Ask: "How much does each degree below 65 add to usage, and each degree above 65? How much did the validation R squared change?"
Look for: both estimates and the change in validation R squared.

3.3 Explore: January
Ask the agent how much power the utility should buy for a January with an average temperature of 30 degrees, from your report in 3.2, at 7 cents a kilowatt hour. Ask it to state the number of households it assumes.

SHARE 4, THE HOMES

4.1 Electric heat in the cold
Add a column: electric_cold, equal to cold_degrees from 3.2 when heating_type starts with Electric and 0 otherwise. Refit the regression of usage_kwh.
Ask: "How much more power does an electrically heated home use in a cold month? What does that mean for its bill at 13.2 cents a kilowatt hour?"
Look for: the estimate for electric_cold; the bill in dollars.

4.2 Older homes
Fit and save: the tree of usage_kwh with home_age_years among the inputs.
Ask: "Do older homes use more power in cold months than newer ones of the same size?"
Look for: splits that combine home age and temperature.

4.3 Explore: home size
Ask the agent whether usage rises steadily with home_sqft or levels off, using the splits in the tree.
