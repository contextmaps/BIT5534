SHARE 1, THE STARTING WAGE (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 Pay against the market
Add a column: wage_gap, equal to starting_wage_usd minus local_market_wage_usd. Fit the logistic regression of quit_6mo in reg_explorer.py with it.
Ask: "How much does each dollar of starting wage above or below the local market change the chance of quitting within six months, with the other inputs held fixed? A dollar more costs about 1,100 dollars a year for an associate working 22 hours, and replacing an associate costs about 1,400 dollars. Is a dollar more worth it?"
Look for: the estimate for wage_gap turned into chances of quitting; the trade with the arithmetic shown.

1.2 Below the market
Fit and save: the tree of quit_6mo with wage_gap among the inputs.
Ask: "Is there a split on wage_gap, and how far below the market can the wage be before quits climb?"
Look for: the split value and the share of quits on each side; a wage rule by region.

1.3 LIVE DATA: the job market
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve the latest unemployment rate, and to say whether, given that rate, the HR director should raise the starting wage beyond your team's finding in 1.2. New hires leave sooner when jobs are easy to find.

SHARE 2, THE SCHEDULE (decision 2)

2.1 Hours short of the request
Add a column: hours_short, equal to hours_requested minus hours_scheduled. Fit the tree of quit_6mo with it among the inputs.
Ask: "Is there a split on hours_short, and how far short of the requested hours can a manager schedule before quits climb?"
Look for: the split value and the share of quits on each side.

2.2 What a short schedule costs
Fit and save: the logistic regression of quit_6mo with hours_short.
Ask: "At 1,400 dollars to replace an associate, what does scheduling a new hire 10 hours short of the request cost in expected quits?"
Look for: the arithmetic with the estimate.

2.3 Explore: experienced store managers
Ask the agent whether new hires quit less under managers with longer tenure, using the splits on manager_tenure_years, and what that means for where new hires are placed.

SHARE 3, THE SEASONAL PLAN (decision 3)

3.1 How many to hire
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on quit_6mo. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "A store needs 20 seasonal associates still working through the holidays. Using the share of hires who quit in our data, how many should it hire? Which hires does the model rank as most likely to stay?"
Look for: the arithmetic for the number to hire; the lift in the threshold report; the agent saying it describes a chance for similar hires.

3.2 The commute
Fit and save: the tree of quit_6mo with commute_miles among the inputs.
Ask: "Is there a split on commute_miles, and at how many miles do quits climb?"
Look for: the split value; what it means for hiring for the busy season.

3.3 Explore: a long commute and low pay
Add a column that multiplies a flag for a commute over your split value by a flag for a wage more than a dollar below the market. Refit and ask the agent whether the two together raise quits more than either alone.

SHARE 4, SALES PER HOUR (your number target)

4.3 Explore: store types
