SHARE 1, THE REMINDER (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 A call or a text
Fit and save: the logistic regression of missed_next in reg_explorer.py, with the inputs your team used in progress report 1.
Ask: "How much lower is the chance of a miss after a phone call than after a text, with the other inputs held fixed? A call costs about 4 dollars and a text about 10 cents, and a missed visit loses 115 dollars. For a patient with what chance of a miss does a call pay for itself?"
Look for: the estimate for Phone call against Text, turned from odds into a change in the chance of a miss; the break even chance, with the arithmetic shown; the limit in your brief that reminders were not assigned at random.

1.2 No reminder at all
Fit and save: the same report as 1.1.
Ask: "How much higher is the chance of a miss when the front desk sends no reminder? How many texts does one prevented miss pay for, and is there any patient a 10 cent text is not worth sending to?"
Look for: the estimate for No reminder; the arithmetic for one prevented miss against the cost of a text.

1.3 Explore: the time of day
next_visit_slot is a lever. Fit the tree of missed_next with next_visit_slot and patient_age among the inputs. Ask the agent which patients miss most in the early morning and evening slots, and whether the front desk should offer them a midday slot or call them instead.

SHARE 2, THE CALL LIST (decision 2)

2.1 Who gets the 15 calls
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on missed_next. Press Save probabilities, open the saved file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "Each clinic can make about 15 calls a day. If the front desk calls the patients with the highest predicted chance of a miss, what share of the misses does it reach with the top tenth of patients? What does that list save at 4 dollars a call against 115 dollars a miss?"
Look for: the lift in the top tenth; the true and false positives at the cutoff that gives that list; the savings, with the arithmetic shown.

2.2 Distance on a wet day
Add a column: wet_distance, equal to distance_miles times forecast_precip_pct divided by 100. Refit the logistic regression with it.
Ask: "Compare the report with wet_distance and the report without it. Does distance raise the chance of a miss more when rain or snow is forecast? What does that mean for the call list at the Floyd clinic, which draws patients from a wide rural area?"
Look for: the estimate for wet_distance and its logworth; whether the validation AUC rose; a concrete change to the call list on a wet day.

2.3 LIVE DATA: tomorrow's call list
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve tomorrow's forecast for Floyd and for Blacksburg, and to say how your team's findings change tomorrow's call list at each clinic.
Look for: the forecast values with their source and date; a recommendation built on your team's own report numbers.

SHARE 3, DOUBLE BOOKING (decision 3)

3.1 When double booking pays
Fit and save: the threshold report from item 2.1, or your own.
Ask: "When we double book a slot and both patients come, we pay about 45 dollars of overtime. When one of them misses, the slot is filled and we lose nothing instead of 115 dollars. At what chance of a miss does double booking pay? How many Validation patients are above that chance, and how many of them missed?"
Look for: the break even chance, with the arithmetic shown; the threshold explorer set to that chance; the confusion matrix at that cutoff.

3.2 A freezing morning
Fit and save: the tree of missed_next with forecast_low_f among the inputs.
Ask: "Is there a split on forecast_low_f in the tree, and at what temperature? Does the chance of a miss rise steadily as the forecast gets colder, or below some temperature?"
Look for: the split value and the size of the groups on each side; whether the logistic regression's single slope for forecast_low_f misses the pattern.

3.3 LIVE DATA: how many slots tomorrow
After Tuesday's class, ask the agent for tomorrow's forecast low and chance of rain or snow at each clinic, and how many slots each clinic should double book, from your team's findings in 3.1 and 3.2.
