SHARE 1, THE ESCROW HOLD (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 Cross border payments
Add a column: xborder_bank, equal to 1 when cross_border is Yes and payment_method is Bank transfer, and 0 otherwise. Fit the logistic regression of dispute in reg_explorer.py with it.
Ask: "Do cross border projects paid by bank transfer dispute more than other projects, with the other inputs held fixed? How much higher is their chance of a dispute?"
Look for: the estimate for xborder_bank turned into a chance of a dispute; whether the validation AUC rose over the model without it.

1.2 Is a longer hold worth it
Fit and save: the same report as 1.1.
Ask: "Holding a cross border payment 7 more days costs about 1 percent of its value. A dispute costs about 140 dollars of staff time plus the 12 percent fee on the refunded share. For a 1,000 dollar project, at what chance of a dispute does the longer hold pay? Our brief gives no figure for how many disputes a hold prevents, so show the answer for a quarter and for a half."
Look for: two break even chances with the arithmetic shown; the agent saying what it cannot know from the brief.

1.3 LIVE DATA: today's rates
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve today's rates for INR, PHP, PLN and BRL, and to say how much of a cross border payment Taskmeadow should hold this week, from your findings in 1.1 and 1.2.

SHARE 2, THE EARLY CHECK (decision 2)

2.1 Which projects get a check
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on dispute. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "An early check costs about 12 dollars and prevents about a third of the disputes it catches. At what predicted chance of a dispute does a check pay, for a project of a typical budget? How many Validation projects are above that chance, and how many of them disputed?"
Look for: the break even chance, with the arithmetic shown; the confusion matrix at that cutoff.

2.2 Clients with a history
Fit and save: the tree of dispute with client_past_disputes among the inputs.
Ask: "How much does each earlier dispute raise a client's chance of another? Does the risk keep rising with each one, or does it level off?"
Look for: the splits on client_past_disputes and the share of disputes in each group.

2.3 Explore: freelancer ratings
Ask the agent whether a lower freelancer rating raises the chance of a dispute steadily or below some rating, using the splits on freelancer_rating, and whether the early check should look at the rating.

SHARE 3, THE REVISION RULE (decision 3)

3.1 Revisions and payout
Fit and save: the regression and the tree of payout_usd.
Ask: "How does payout change with the number of revisions, with the budget held fixed? Is there a split on revisions_requested past which the extra revisions are unpaid work?"
Look for: the estimate and the split value; what the extra revisions cost a freelancer.

3.2 Revisions and disputes
Fit and save: the logistic regression of dispute.
Ask: "How much does each revision raise the chance of a dispute? Would capping free revisions at three pay for itself, at 140 dollars a dispute?"
Look for: the estimate; the arithmetic for the cap, with the agent stating what it assumes about clients who would have asked for more.

3.3 Explore: rush projects with many revisions
Add a column that multiplies revisions_requested by a flag for a deadline under 7 days. Refit the logistic regression of dispute and ask the agent whether rush projects with many revisions dispute more than either one alone.

SHARE 4, DEADLINES AND BUDGETS

4.1 Rush work
Add a column: budget_per_day, equal to budget_usd divided by deadline_days. Refit the logistic regression of dispute with it.
Ask: "Do projects with a large budget for a short deadline dispute more? When should Taskmeadow warn a client that a deadline is short for the budget?"
Look for: the estimate for budget_per_day; a warning rule; the limit in your brief that a short deadline does not cause a dispute by itself.

4.2 Categories
Fit and save: the logistic regression of dispute with category among the inputs.
Ask: "Do some categories of work dispute more, with the other inputs held fixed? If the estimates are small, say so."
Look for: the category estimates and their logworth.

4.3 Explore: the money in disputes
With your dispute and payout reports attached, ask the agent what disputes cost Taskmeadow in a year of 5,000 projects, and which lever in your reports would cut that cost most.
