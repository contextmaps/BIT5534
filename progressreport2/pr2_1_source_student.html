Learning Objectives

By the end of this progress report, each team will be able to:

  1. Divide an investigation of its business among its members and keep track of who covers what.
  2. Combine every member's findings in one thread with its agent, and test where the findings agree, conflict or leave a gap.
  3. Turn the findings into a recommendation for each decision in its brief, with the numbers, the money and the confidence behind it.
  4. Tell the story of its investigation for the business's managers, from the problem to the first action.

Section 2 of 6
THE QUESTIONS

Your team works from two sets of questions, about thirty items in all. The general questions below are the same for every team, in six topics. Your team's own questions are in your team's file, in five shares built from your brief: one share for each of your three decisions, one for your levers and your number target, and one for your text column and the rest of your business.

In each item you will find what to fit and save, the question to ask your agent, and what to look for in the answer. Some items are complete questions. Items marked Explore hold a direction, and you write the questions. Every item is a place to start, so keep asking until you understand the answer and can say what it means for a decision in your business.

Items marked LIVE DATA use your agent's live data connection. Do those items after your instructor covers the live data connection in class.

Class material
Topic 1, which model to trust
Fit and save: all four course models on your yes or no target, with the same inputs and your Validation column. Attach the four reports in one message.

Ask: "Here are the reports of all four models on our yes or no target. Which model ranks the Yes rows best on the Validation rows, by AUC and by the lift in the top tenth? Is the difference large enough to change any of our decisions?"

Ask: "Compare the train and validation numbers in each report. Which model fits the Training rows much better than the Validation rows, and what does that mean for using it on next month's rows?"

Look for: AUC and lift on the Validation rows. When Yes is rare, misclassification at the 0.5 cutoff barely differs between models, so it tells you little. A large gap between train and validation means the model was fitted to noise in the Training rows. The choice of a model should be tied to a decision in your brief.

Explore: Do the same for your number target with validation R squared. If a different model wins each target, ask the agent which model your team should use for each decision, and why.
Class material
Topic 2, the column recorded after the outcome
Fit and save: one model on your yes or no target twice, once without the column your team named in its data profile and once with it.

Ask: "These two reports differ only by the column recorded after the outcome. How much does adding it raise the validation AUC, and why can our business not use the second model when it makes its decisions?"

Ask: "If a manager trusted the second model, which of our decisions would go wrong, and what would that cost with our planning figures?"

Look for: an answer that compares when the column is recorded with when the decision is made; the agent declining to recommend the second model; a cost in dollars with the arithmetic shown.

Explore: Ask the agent whether any other column in your data file might be recorded after a decision is made. Check its answer against the column descriptions in your brief.
Class material
Topic 3, levers, facts and the limits in your brief
Fit and save: the regression of your number target and the logistic regression of your yes or no target.

Ask: "For each lever in our brief, what do these reports say about how it goes with each target, with the other inputs held fixed? Turn each into dollars with our planning figures, and show the arithmetic."

Ask: "Can we say from these reports that changing a lever will change the outcome? What would our business need to know, or do, before it could say so?"

Look for: each estimate with its logworth and its report section; dollars with the arithmetic shown; the difference between an association with the other inputs held fixed and the effect of changing a lever.

Explore: Your brief lists conclusions the agent may not draw. Ask the agent to draw one of them, the way a manager in a hurry might, and see whether it keeps to your brief. If it gives in, tell it which part of the brief it broke and ask again.
Class material
Topic 4, where a straight line misses
Fit and save: the regression and the tree on the same target. Attach both reports.

Ask: "Compare the regression and the tree. Where is there a split in the tree at a value of an input, such as a threshold or a turn, that the regression's single slope for that input cannot capture?"

Ask: "For the first two splits in the tree, which rows fall on each side, how different are they in the target, and which of our decisions does each split matter for?"

Look for: split values taken from the tree report; the number of rows in each group; a threshold a manager can act on.

Explore: When a split on one input sits inside a split on another, the effect of one input depends on the other. Add a column in Excel that multiplies the two, refit the regression with it, and ask the agent whether the validation number changed and what the new estimate means.
Class material
Topic 5, test what your brief says
Fit and save: the reports your team needs for the statement you choose.

Ask: "Our brief says: [paste one sentence from THE BUSINESS section of your brief]. Do our reports support that? Give the numbers and their report sections, or say which report we would need."

Ask: "Which statement in our brief do our reports not support, or support only for some rows?"

Look for: a statement checked number by number; an answer that says when the reports do not cover a statement.

Explore: Pick the statement your managers would act on first, fit the model that tests it most directly, and bring the report back to the agent.
Class material
Topic 6, how far the findings travel
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most for each target.

Ask: "Our data file covers the dates in our brief. Which of our findings could depend on the season or the year, and how could we check with the columns we have?"

Ask: "Which group in our tree reports is small enough that its numbers may not hold next year? Use the counts in the reports."

Look for: counts of rows; the gap between the Training and Validation rows; the difference between a group's average and one customer, which your brief also notes.

Explore: Ask the agent what result in a future month would make your team change its main recommendation, and how your team would notice it.

Class material
Questions to start the team conversation
Group our insight lines by the decisions in our brief. For each decision, which lines bear on it, and what do they say together?

Where do two of our findings disagree, or point to different actions? What could explain the difference, and which report would settle it?

Which of our findings rests on the strongest evidence, and which on the weakest? Judge by the Validation rows, the number of rows behind each finding and the gap between train and validation.

For each decision in our brief, what is still missing from our lines, and which model or question would fill the gap?

How do our decisions affect each other? If the manager acts on one finding, does that change another decision?

Put our findings in dollars where our planning figures allow. Which finding is worth the most to the business, and how sure are we of it?

If our manager could act on one thing next month, what should it be, and what should the manager watch to know whether it worked?
Ask them in any order, in your own words, and add your own.
