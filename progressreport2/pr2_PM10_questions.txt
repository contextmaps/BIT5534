SHARE 1, THE CURE SETTINGS (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 Cure time and humidity
Fit and save: the regression of tensile_strength_mpa in reg_explorer.py, with the inputs your team used in progress report 1.
Ask: "How much does each extra 10 minutes of cure time add to tensile strength, and how much does each point of humidity take away, with the other inputs held fixed?"
Look for: both estimates with their report section; what they mean in MPa for a typical batch.

1.2 A longer cure on a humid day
Add a column humid, equal to 1 when ambient_humidity_pct is above 60, and a column humid_time, equal to humid times cure_time_min. Refit the regression of tensile_strength_mpa and the logistic regression of rejected.
Ask: "Does a longer cure protect a batch on a humid day? Ten more minutes cost about 120 dollars, and a rejected batch costs about 3,800. How much longer should the cure run when humidity is above 60, and is it worth it?"
Look for: the estimates for humid and humid_time; the trade with the arithmetic shown.

1.3 LIVE DATA: tomorrow's humidity
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve tomorrow's humidity forecast for the plant, and to recommend tomorrow's cure times from your finding in 1.2.

SHARE 2, THE RESIN SUPPLIER (decision 2)

2.1 The resin ratio
Fit and save: the regression and the tree of tensile_strength_mpa.
Ask: "Does strength rise steadily with resin_ratio_pct, or is there a best ratio? Use the splits in the tree."
Look for: the regression slope against the splits; the ratio that gives the strongest panels.

2.2 Suppliers
Fit and save: the logistic regression of rejected.
Ask: "Do batches from one supplier get rejected more often, with the other inputs held fixed? At 3,800 dollars a rejected batch, how much would that supplier have to discount its resin to be worth keeping?"
Look for: the supplier estimates; the arithmetic, with the number of batches a year stated as an assumption.

2.3 Explore: a bad lot
Ask the agent whether your team can tell, from these reports, a supplier with a bad lot from a supplier with worse resin overall, and check that it keeps to the limit in your brief about fault.

SHARE 3, THE EXTRA TEST (decision 3)

3.1 Which batches get a test
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on rejected. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "An extra lab test costs about 90 dollars and catches most weak batches. A weak batch that reaches a customer costs about 15,000 dollars. At what predicted chance of a rejection should a batch get the test? How many Validation batches are above it, and how many were rejected?"
Look for: the break even chance with the arithmetic shown; the confusion matrix at that cutoff.

3.2 Operator experience
Fit and save: the tree of tensile_strength_mpa with operator_experience_years among the inputs.
Ask: "Does strength rise with operator experience, and does each year add the same amount?"
Look for: the splits; the limit in your brief that experience is a column and not a judgment of effort.

3.3 Explore: lines and shifts
Ask the agent whether some lines or shifts carry more rejections with the other inputs held fixed, and whether the extra test should target them.

SHARE 4, WHAT MAKES A PANEL STRONG (your number target)

4.1 Temperature and time together
Add a column: cure_index, equal to cure_temp_c times cure_time_min divided by 100. Refit the regression of tensile_strength_mpa.
Ask: "Does the combination of temperature and time explain strength better than each alone? Does more cure keep adding strength, judging by the splits on cure_index in the tree?"
Look for: the change in validation R squared; splits that show where extra cure stops helping.

4.2 Fiber content
Fit and save: the tree of tensile_strength_mpa.
Ask: "Does strength keep rising with fiber_content_pct, or level off past some content?"
Look for: the splits on fiber_content_pct.

4.3 Explore: line speed
Ask the agent what a faster line costs in strength, and whether your team has evidence for running the line faster on dry days.
