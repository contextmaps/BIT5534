SHARE 1, THE TICKET PRICE (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 Price and the act
Fit and save: the regression of ticket_revenue_usd in reg_explorer.py, with the inputs your team used in progress report 1.
Ask: "How does ticket revenue change with the ticket price and with the act's monthly listeners, with the other inputs held fixed? Each person in the room also spends about 14 dollars at the bars. What does a 5 dollar higher price do to the night's total, tickets and bars together?"
Look for: both estimates; the arithmetic for 5 dollars, with the agent stating how it estimates attendance from revenue.

1.2 A small act
Add a column: price_small_act, equal to ticket_price_usd when monthly_listeners_thousands is below 200 and 0 otherwise. Refit the regression of ticket_revenue_usd.
Ask: "Does a higher price hurt small acts more than well known ones? Is our price too high for a small act?"
Look for: the estimate for price_small_act and whether the validation R squared rose; a price rule by size of act.

1.3 Explore: days on sale
days_on_sale is a lever. Ask the agent whether shows that go on sale earlier sell more, and whether your team can tell that apart, from these reports, from the venue putting its biggest shows on sale first.

SHARE 2, THE MARKETING BUDGET (decision 2)

2.1 Another 1,000 dollars
Fit and save: the regression of ticket_revenue_usd.
Ask: "How much ticket revenue does another 1,000 dollars of marketing add, with the other inputs held fixed? Counting 14 dollars a person at the bars, does it pay?"
Look for: the estimate; the arithmetic; the limit in your brief that the venue spent more on shows it expected to sell.

2.2 Diminishing returns
Fit and save: the tree of ticket_revenue_usd with marketing_usd among the inputs.
Ask: "Does revenue keep rising with marketing, or level off past some amount? What marketing budget would you set for a typical show?"
Look for: the splits on marketing_usd and the revenue in each group.

2.3 Explore: marketing for small and large acts
Ask the agent whether another 1,000 dollars of marketing is worth more for a small act or a well known one, from your reports, and where the marketing manager should spend first.

SHARE 3, THE RESCUE (decision 3)

3.1 Which shows to rescue

Ask: "Discounted tickets sell at 10 percent off and take some full price sales. An empty seat loses the ticket and 14 dollars at the bars. At what chance of selling under 60 percent should the box office release discounted tickets or add marketing? How many Validation shows are above that chance?"
Look for: the break even reasoning with the arithmetic shown and the assumptions named; the counts at that cutoff.

3.2 Competing shows on weeknights
