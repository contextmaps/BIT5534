SHARE 1, THE ASKING RENT (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 What an increase costs
Fit and save: the logistic regression of vacant_45 in reg_explorer.py, with the inputs your team used in progress report 1.
Ask: "How much does each point of asked_increase_pct raise the chance that a unit sits 45 days, with the other inputs held fixed? A day of vacancy costs one thirtieth of the monthly rent plus about 8 dollars. For a 1,200 dollar unit, what does each extra point of increase cost in expected vacancy, against the extra rent over a 12 month lease?"
Look for: the estimate turned from odds into a change in the chance of a long vacancy; both sides of the trade with the arithmetic shown; the limit in your brief that managers asked larger increases in units they thought would fill.

1.2 Above the market
Add a column: ask_vs_market, equal to gap_to_market_pct plus asked_increase_pct. Fit the tree of vacant_45 with it among the inputs.
Ask: "Is there a split on ask_vs_market, and how far above the market can a unit be asked before the chance of a long vacancy climbs?"
Look for: the split value and the share of long vacancies on each side; a rule a manager can use.

1.3 LIVE DATA: rents in general
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve the latest national rent index, and to say whether, given that index, Quarrystep should ask larger or smaller increases next year than your team would ask from your reports alone.

SHARE 2, THE CONCESSION (decision 2)

2.1 When a free month pays
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on vacant_45. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report.
Ask: "A free month costs one month's rent spread over the lease. Our brief gives no figure for how much a concession shortens a vacancy. For a 1,200 dollar unit, show the break even chance of a long vacancy for a concession that saves 15 days and for one that saves 30 days, and count the Validation units above each."
Look for: two break even chances with the arithmetic shown; the counts at each cutoff; the agent saying what it cannot know from the brief.

2.2 The hard months
Fit and save: the logistic regression and the tree of vacant_45 with lease_end_month among the inputs.
Ask: "Which months are the hardest to fill, with the other inputs held fixed? Should Quarrystep time leases to end in summer?"
Look for: the month estimates; a recommendation with its cost.

2.3 Explore: notice
Ask the agent whether a short notice period raises the chance of a long vacancy steadily or below some number of days, using the splits on notice_days, and what that means for the notice Quarrystep asks of tenants.

SHARE 3, THE RENEWAL OFFER (decision 3)

3.1 What a renewal is worth
With your vacancy report attached, ask: "A tenant who renews saves the 900 dollar preparation cost and the vacancy. For a typical unit, what is a renewal worth in dollars? How large an increase could Quarrystep offer a renewing tenant and still come out ahead of a turnover?"
Look for: the expected vacancy cost built from your report, with the arithmetic shown; the agent saying that the data hold turnovers, not renewals, so it cannot say how many tenants accept each offer.

3.2 Renewal by season
Ask: "Using the month estimates from 2.2, should the renewal offer be larger for leases that end in the hard months?"
Look for: a renewal policy that changes by month, built from your estimates.

3.3 Explore: the communities
Ask the agent whether some communities fill slowly with the other inputs held fixed, and whether the renewal offer should differ there.
