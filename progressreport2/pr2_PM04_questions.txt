Some items ask you to add a column to your data file. Open the file in Excel, add the column with the formula described, and save the file as a new .csv under a new name. Keep your original file as it is.

SHARE 1, THE FOLLOW UP SPEED (decision 1 in your brief)

1.1 What a day costs
Fit and save: the logistic regression of signed in reg_explorer.py, with the inputs your team used in progress report 1.
Ask: "How much does each day before the first call lower the chance of signing, with the other inputs held fixed? Brightrafter earns about 18 percent of a contract. For a lead with a typical quote, what does each day of delay cost in expected margin?"
Look for: the estimate turned into chances of signing; the cost of a day with the arithmetic shown.

1.2 The first days
Fit and save: the tree of signed with days_to_first_call among the inputs.
Ask: "Does the chance of signing fall steadily with each day, or fastest in the first days? Calling every lead within a day takes an extra salesperson at about 4,000 dollars a month. Is that worth it?"
Look for: the splits on days_to_first_call; the monthly arithmetic, with the number of leads a month stated as an assumption.

1.3 Explore: who gets called first
Your brief warns that salespeople may call promising leads first. Ask the agent how that would bias the estimate for days_to_first_call, and what Brightrafter could do to learn what speed is really worth.

SHARE 2, THE HOME VISIT (decision 2)

2.1 Which leads get a visit
Fit and save: the model your team trusts most on signed. Press Save probabilities, open the file in the threshold explorer with Rows set to your Validation column, and save a threshold report. Attach the regression of quoted_value_usd as well.
Ask: "A home visit costs about 240 dollars. At 18 percent margin on a typical quote, at what chance of signing does a visit pay? Our brief gives no figure for how much a visit raises the chance of signing, so show the answer for a visit that adds 5 points and one that adds 10 points. How many Validation leads are above each?"
Look for: two break even chances with the arithmetic shown; the counts at each cutoff.

2.2 Credit bands
Fit and save: the tree of signed with credit_band among the inputs.
Ask: "Do leads with Fair or Poor credit sign far less often? Should they receive a visit?"
Look for: the split on credit_band and the share of signed leads on each side.

2.3 LIVE DATA: sunlight
After your instructor covers the live data connection in class, ask the agent to retrieve the week's solar radiation for your five counties, and to say whether it should change which leads receive a visit.

SHARE 3, THE FINANCING OFFER (decision 3)

3.1 Credit and signing
Fit and save: the logistic regression of signed.
Ask: "How much does each credit band change the chance of signing, with the other inputs held fixed? What does that say about which homeowners need financing to sign?"
Look for: the credit band estimates; the agent keeping to the limit in your brief on a homeowner's finances.

3.2 Weak credit and a valuable home
Add a column: weak_credit_value, equal to home_value_usd divided by 1,000 when credit_band is Fair or Poor and 0 otherwise. Refit the logistic regression of signed.
Ask: "Do homeowners with weak credit and a valuable home sign more often than others with weak credit? What financing would you offer them?"
Look for: the estimate for the new column; the agent saying that the brief gives no financing rates.

3.3 Explore: the size of the system
Ask the agent whether larger systems sign less often, using your quote and signing reports together, and whether financing should depend on the size of the system.

SHARE 4, THE QUOTE (your number target)

4.1 The bill and the roof
Fit and save: the regression and the tree of quoted_value_usd.
Ask: "How much does the monthly bill add to the quote, and how much does the roof area add? Which one limits the system for most homes, judging by the splits in the tree?"

4.2 Shade
Fit and save: the same tree.
Ask: "Does the quote fall steadily with shading_pct, or past some level of shade? How much smaller is the system on a shaded roof?"

4.3 Explore: facing south
Ask the agent what a south facing roof adds to the quote and to the chance of signing, and whether the sales team should favor those leads.
