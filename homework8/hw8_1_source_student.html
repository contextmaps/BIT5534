Choosing a Threshold for a Breakdown Model
Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Read a confusion matrix at a threshold and say what each of its four counts means for a delivery fleet.
  2. Read precision, recall, F1 and accuracy at two thresholds, and explain why accuracy can stay level while the other three move.
  3. Find the threshold with the largest F1 on validation rows, carry it to test rows, and say which test figure to report.
  4. Find the ROC optimal threshold and compare the number of vans each threshold sends to the garage.
  5. Say which threshold fits a fleet when a missed breakdown costs about the same as an unneeded inspection, less, or more.

Part 1 is about choosing a threshold, from the week 9 lectures, on one model and one file. In case one you read the confusion matrix and F1. In case two you read the ROC curve and decide which threshold fits which kind of month.

The file is fleet_saved.csv from Thistledown Parcel, a delivery company made up for this homework. Each row is one van at the start of a month. Breakdown is Yes when the van broke down on a route within the next 30 days, and No otherwise. The fleet manager wants a model that says which vans to send to the garage for an inspection before they break down.

The four probability columns are already in the file, so you fit nothing in this homework. The week 8 tools made them at their default settings, the same four base models you saved in case one of homework 7 part 2. REG prob Yes is the logistic regression, and part 1 uses only that column. Every row is marked validation or test in the Sample column, 4200 validation rows and 1800 test rows. A validation row's probability comes from a model that did not see that row, and a test row's probability comes from models fitted without any test rows.

The probabilities are in the file, so every figure is the same on every machine, and the checks on this page give them exactly. A figure that differs from a check most likely means Rows is set to the wrong sample.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Open fleet_saved.csv in threshold_explorer.py. The window opens with Actual set to Breakdown, Probability set to REG prob Yes, Positive class set to Yes, Rows set to Sample and equal to set to test. Change equal to from test to validation. Every row at or above the threshold is predicted Yes, a van the model would send to the garage.

Check three things. The status line reads 4200 rows. The Tables tab at threshold 0.5 shows TP 317, FP 112, FN 351 and TN 3420, and a row sum of 668 for actual Yes. The Curves tab prints largest F1 0.632 at threshold 0.3882, in purple under the five values.

Now record three thresholds. At 0.5 on the validation rows, write down the four counts, precision, recall, F1 and accuracy from the Tables tab, where recall is shown as TPR. Then type 0.3882 into the Threshold box, press Enter, and write down the same figures again. The Curves tab named 0.3882 as the threshold with the largest F1.

Last, change equal to from validation to test, leave the threshold at 0.3882, and write down the same figures for the test rows. The Curves tab now prints the largest F1 of the test rows and its own threshold. Write that down too, and leave the threshold where it is.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one frames the two errors AND move two reads the matrix at 0.5 AND move three reads the largest F1 threshold AND move four carries that threshold to the test rows. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you read a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested. Before any number, say what a false negative and a false positive mean for Thistledown, a van that broke down on a route without being sent to the garage, and a healthy van taken off the road for an inspection it did not need. Say which of the two errors you think is worse, and why. Ask Hokie AI to argue the other side.

Move two reads the matrix at 0.5 on the validation rows. Report the four counts, precision and recall, and say in the fleet's words what each of those two rates means. Say what a recall of that size means for the vans that will break down this month.

Move three reads the threshold with the largest F1. Report the same figures at 0.3882, and say how many more breakdowns the lower threshold catches and how many more healthy vans it sends to the garage. Report accuracy at both thresholds, and say what accuracy does not show here.

Move four carries 0.3882 to the test rows. Report the test figures at 0.3882 beside the test rows' own largest F1 and its threshold. Say which F1 you would give the fleet manager as the model's expected performance, and why the other one would overstate it. Defend the choice against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

In case one you found the threshold with the largest F1. In case two you find a second candidate on the ROC curve, and decide which threshold fits which kind of month at Thistledown. Change equal to back to validation, and open the ROC tab. It draws TPR against FPR over every threshold, prints the AUC, and marks the optimal threshold with a green square, the point furthest above the diagonal.

Check two things. The ROC tab prints AUC 0.893 and the optimal threshold 0.1716, with FPR 0.167 and TPR 0.811. Type 0.1716 into the Threshold box and press Enter, and the Tables tab shows TP 542, FP 589, FN 126 and TN 2943.

You now have three thresholds for the same model, 0.5, 0.3882 and 0.1716. For each one, write down from the Tables tab the vans sent to the garage, which is TP plus FP, the breakdowns caught, which is TP, and the healthy vans sent, which is FP.

Each threshold rests on an assumption about the two errors. When a missed breakdown and an unneeded inspection cost about the same, the best threshold is the one with the fewest errors in total, which is where the accuracy curve on the Curves tab is highest. The ROC optimal threshold weighs one missed breakdown as heavily as about five unneeded inspections, because TPR is counted over the 668 vans that broke down and FPR over the 3532 that did not, and 3532 is about five times 668. The largest F1 has no such reading, because F1 never counts TN, the healthy vans left on the road.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one reads the ROC optimum AND move two says what each threshold assumes AND move three works through the three situations AND move four recommends a threshold for Thistledown's usual month. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Report the AUC, the optimal threshold with its TPR and FPR, and its four counts. Report the vans sent to the garage, the breakdowns caught and the healthy vans sent at 0.5, at 0.3882 and at 0.1716. Ask Hokie AI to check that your counts agree with your TPR and FPR.

Move two says, in your own words, what each of the three thresholds assumes about a missed breakdown against an unneeded inspection, using the paragraph above. Find on the Curves tab where the accuracy curve is highest, and say which of the three thresholds sits closest to it.

Move three takes the three situations on the card one at a time. For each one, say whether you would move the threshold up or down from 0.5, and which of the three thresholds comes closest to what that situation needs, and why. Ask Hokie AI to argue against your answer for the situation you are least sure of.

Move four recommends one threshold for Thistledown's usual month. Say which situation you think a usual month is closest to, and why, then give the threshold, its counts and the vans it sends to the garage. Defend the choice against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

One move is not one message. Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.
