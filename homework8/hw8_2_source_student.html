Comparing Four Models by AUC and Lift
Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Read the AUC and the ROC optimal point of four models, and say in words what an AUC means.
  2. Explain how a model with few distinct probabilities can have a low AUC and a high F1 at one threshold.
  3. Judge which differences in AUC hold from validation rows to test rows.
  4. Read lift at a fixed share of rows, and count the positives a fixed capacity finds against a random choice.
  5. Explain why two models with the same AUC can find different numbers of positives at the same capacity, and recommend a model and a rule for a monthly list.

Part 2 is about comparing models, from the week 9 lectures, on one file. In case one you compare four models by their ROC curves and AUC. In case two you compare them by lift at the number of vans a garage can inspect.

The file is fleet_saved.csv from Thistledown Parcel, a delivery company made up for this homework. Each row is one van at the start of a month. Breakdown is Yes when the van broke down on a route within the next 30 days, and No otherwise. The fleet manager wants a model that says which vans to send to the garage for an inspection before they break down, and the garage can inspect about one van in ten each month.

The four probability columns are already in the file, so you fit nothing in this homework. The week 8 tools made them at their default settings, the same four base models you saved in case one of homework 7 part 2. REG prob Yes is the logistic regression, TREE prob Yes the decision tree, NN prob Yes the neural network and SVM prob Yes the support vector machine. Every row is marked validation or test in the Sample column, 4200 validation rows and 1800 test rows. A validation row's probability comes from a model that did not see that row, and a test row's probability comes from models fitted without any test rows.

The probabilities are in the file, so every figure is the same on every machine, and the checks on this page give them exactly. A figure that differs from a check most likely means Rows is set to the wrong sample, or Probability to the wrong column.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Open fleet_saved.csv in threshold_explorer.py. The window opens with Actual set to Breakdown, Probability set to REG prob Yes, Positive class set to Yes, Rows set to Sample and equal to set to test. Change equal to from test to validation. You will change Probability from one model to the next, and the tool redraws every tab for the column you choose.

Check three things. The status line reads 4200 rows. With REG prob Yes, the ROC tab prints AUC 0.893 and the optimal threshold 0.1716. With TREE prob Yes, the status line counts 25 distinct probabilities and the ROC tab prints AUC 0.883.

For each of the four Probability columns on the validation rows, write down the AUC and the optimal threshold with its TPR and FPR from the ROC tab, the largest F1 and its threshold from the purple line on the Curves tab, and the number of distinct probabilities from the status line.

Then change equal to from validation to test and write down the AUC of each of the four models on the test rows.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one reports the four AUCs AND move two reads the tree AND move three carries the AUCs to the test rows AND move four names the model that ranks the vans best. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you read a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested. Report the four validation AUCs, each with its optimal threshold, TPR and FPR. Say in words what the highest AUC means for two vans picked at random, one that broke down and one that did not.

Move two reads the tree. Report its distinct probabilities beside the other three models', and describe what its ROC curve looks like on the ROC tab. Report the largest F1 of all four models and its threshold. The tree has the lowest AUC on the validation rows. Say how it can also have the highest F1, and why its optimal threshold and its largest F1 threshold are the same.

Move three carries the comparison to the test rows. Report the four test AUCs, say which part of the validation order held and which part changed, and say what that suggests about AUC differences of about 0.01.

Move four names the model that ranks the vans best overall, with the figures behind it, and says how sure you can be. Say what the AUC cannot tell the fleet manager about a garage that inspects one van in ten. Defend your answer against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

In case one you compared the four models over every threshold at once. In case two you compare them at the share of the fleet the garage inspects. The garage can inspect about one van in ten each month, which is 420 of the 4200 validation vans, so the question is how many breakdowns each model puts among the vans it sends first.

Change equal to back to validation. For each model, choose its Probability column, type its threshold below into the Threshold box and press Enter. Each threshold is the highest one at which PP% on the Lift tab first reaches 10 percent, so the vans at or above it are the ones the model would send. REG prob Yes 0.5084, TREE prob Yes 0.7347, NN prob Yes 0.7390, SVM prob Yes 0.6232.

Check one thing. REG prob Yes at 0.5084 shows 420 rows predicted Yes on the Tables tab, the column sum of predicted Yes, and TP 311.

For each model at its threshold, write down from the Lift tab the PP% and the lift, and from the Tables tab the vans sent, which is the column sum of predicted Yes, the breakdowns found, which is TP, and the precision. A random choice of 420 vans would find about 67 breakdowns, because 668 of the 4200 vans broke down.

With TREE prob Yes, also press Higher once from 0.7347 and write down the vans sent and TP at the new threshold.

Then change equal to from validation to test. For each model, choose its Probability column again and type the same threshold you used on the validation rows, then write down the vans sent and TP on the 1800 test rows. One van in ten of the test rows is 180 vans.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one reports lift at one van in ten AND move two sets the regression beside the support vector machine AND move three reads the tree's steps AND move four carries the thresholds to the test rows and recommends. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Report, for each of the four models, the vans sent, the breakdowns found, the precision and the lift. Say what the best lift means for the garage, in breakdowns found per 420 inspections against about 67 for a random choice.

Move two sets the regression beside the support vector machine. Their validation AUCs are 0.893 and 0.891. Report how many breakdowns each finds in its 420 vans, and say what the difference says about AUC as a guide for a garage with a fixed number of inspections.

Move three reads the tree. Report the vans it sends at 0.7347 and after one press of Higher, and say why the tree cannot send exactly 420. Compare the tree with the network on breakdowns found and on precision, and say which you would rather give the garage at this capacity.

Move four carries each model's validation threshold to the test rows. Report the vans each model sends and the breakdowns it finds there, against the 180 the garage can inspect. Recommend one model to the fleet manager and a rule for choosing the vans each month, a fixed threshold or the highest probabilities up to the garage's capacity, with the figures behind both choices. Defend the recommendation against Hokie AI's strongest objection.
