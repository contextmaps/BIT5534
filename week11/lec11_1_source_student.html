  1. Describe the team project, a browser dashboard of the course's models with an analytics agent beside it, and how the instructor assigns the twenty projects.

📋 Note for Students

Homework 9 was the last homework. From today the team project is the main work of the course, and each week you add one capability to it.

In earlier years students wrote a technical report. This year each team builds a browser dashboard of this course's models, on one made up business, with the team's Hokie AI agent open beside it. A manager reviews the results and asks the agent what they mean. The agent also retrieves one kind of live public data, such as a forecast, an exchange rate or an interest rate, and a manager's decision can depend on its current value.

Sixteen teams choose from twenty projects, and no two teams share a project. Each team lists five in order of preference, and your instructor assigns projects first come, first served. Open any project to read its description. Every dataset has several thousand rows, continuous and categorical inputs, a number target, a yes or no target whose yes class is the minority, a text column and a Validation column. The businesses are made up, and your instructor built each dataset to resemble real data from its industry.

The twenty projects. Open one to read it.
1. Meal kit subscribers

A regional meal kit company ships weekly boxes of recipes and ingredients. Each row is one subscriber, with tenure, how often they skip a week, average recipe rating, delivery delays, the share of orders bought with a discount, household size, plan type, region and how they first signed up. The number target is the subscriber's spending over the next quarter. The yes or no target is whether the subscriber cancels within 90 days, which about one subscriber in seven does. Each row includes the subscriber's most recent note to customer service. Grocery prices change every month, and the company has to decide how large a price increase its subscribers will accept, so the agent retrieves a current food price index and compares it with the models' estimates of price sensitivity.

2. International apparel orders

An online clothing retailer sells to customers in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and the euro area, and offers free returns everywhere. Each row is one order, with item price, discount, number of items, whether the sizes in the order are consistent, the customer's past return rate, shipping days, country, product category and whether the customer used the size guide. The number target is the order's margin after any return. The yes or no target is whether the customer returns the order, which happens in close to one order in five. Each row includes the customer's product review. The retailer pays refunds in local currency, so the dollar cost of a return depends on the day's exchange rate, and the agent retrieves current rates so the team can judge whether free returns remain profitable in each market.

3. Fleet auto insurance claims

An insurer covers delivery vans and service trucks owned by small businesses. Each row is one claim, with vehicle age and mileage, the repair estimate, days between the accident and the report, prior claims on the policy, driver experience, vehicle make and model, claim type and state. The number target is the final claim cost, which has a long tail of very expensive claims. The yes or no target is whether the insurer flagged the claim as likely fraud, about one claim in sixteen. Each row includes the adjuster's notes. Manufacturers issue safety recalls by model, and an adjuster reviews a claim on a vehicle with an open recall differently, so the agent looks up current recalls before the team routes a claim to the fraud unit or pays it.

4. Residential solar leads

A residential solar installer collects leads from its website and from door to door canvassing. Each row is one lead, with roof area and pitch, the monthly electric bill, home value, credit band, a shading estimate, days since first contact, lead source, home type and county. The number target is the quoted contract value. The yes or no target is whether the homeowner signs, about one lead in eight. Each row includes the salesperson's call notes. A system's output depends on sunlight at the address and its cost depends on financing rates, so the agent retrieves sunlight data for the location and current interest rates, and the sales team uses both to choose which homeowners receive a site visit and which financing to offer.

5. Regional airline flights

A regional airline flies small jets between hub airports and smaller cities. Each row is one flight, with scheduled departure hour, distance, aircraft age, the delay of the aircraft's previous flight, load factor, crew duty hours, origin, destination, day of week and season. The number target is the arrival delay in minutes, which grows as the day goes on. The yes or no target is whether the airline cancels the flight or lands it more than two hours late, about one flight in eleven. Each row includes the dispatcher's operations remarks. Much of the disruption comes from weather at the two airports, so the agent retrieves tomorrow's forecast at each airport, and the operations team uses it to decide where to hold a spare aircraft and crew.

6. Quick service restaurant sites

A sandwich franchise is opening locations across the Southeast and wants to know which candidate sites are likely to be profitable. Each row is one existing location, with seating, drive through lanes, traffic count on the street, distance to the nearest competitor, years open, rent per square foot, local wage, site type and state. The number target is annual sales. The yes or no target is whether the franchise closed the location within two years of opening, about one in ten. Each row includes the franchise consultant's notes from the site visit. Income and population around an address are available from public census data, which the agent looks up for each new candidate site before the team recommends approving it.

7. Physical therapy appointments

A chain of physical therapy clinics loses revenue every time a patient misses a visit. Each row is one course of treatment, with patient age, distance from the clinic, visits prescribed, days between referral and first visit, copay, prior missed visits, injury type, insurance type and clinic. The number target is the number of visits the patient completed. The yes or no target is whether the patient missed the next scheduled visit, about one in eight. Each row includes the front desk's scheduling notes. Patients miss more visits in bad weather, so the agent retrieves the forecast for each clinic, and the clinic staff use it to decide how many reminder calls to make and how much to double book.

8. Electric utility households

A municipal electric utility serves homes in a region with cold winters and hot summers. Each row is one household in one month, with home size, home age, heating type, occupants, the month's average temperature, past late payments, budget billing enrollment, rate plan and neighborhood. The number target is the month's usage in kilowatt hours. The yes or no target is whether the household left the bill unpaid for more than 60 days, about one household in eleven. Each row includes notes from the household's last service call. Bills rise sharply in electrically heated homes during a cold snap, so the agent retrieves the coming week's forecast, and the utility uses it to choose which households receive a payment plan offer before it sends the bills.

9. Commercial building energy

An energy services firm manages office buildings, laboratories and warehouses for corporate clients. Each row is one building on one day, with floor area, occupancy, the outdoor high temperature, humidity, the age of the cooling plant, whether staff used lab equipment, the thermostat setpoint, building type and day of week. The number target is the day's peak electrical demand in kilowatts. The yes or no target is whether that peak exceeded the building's contracted peak, which happens on about one day in twelve, and on such a day the client pays a large demand charge. Each row includes the facilities manager's log. Demand climbs steeply in hot weather, so the agent retrieves tomorrow's temperature forecast, and the facilities team uses it to decide whether to precool the building overnight or reduce load in the afternoon.

10. Home listings

A real estate brokerage advises sellers on list prices in a metro area with distinct neighborhoods. Each row is one listing, with square footage, lot size, bedrooms, bathrooms, age, distance to downtown, school rating, years since the last renovation, neighborhood, property type and listing month. The number target is the sale price. The yes or no target is whether the home was still unsold 90 days after listing, about one in seven. Each row includes the listing description written by the seller's realtor. Buyers can afford less when mortgage rates rise, so the agent retrieves current interest rates, and the brokerage adjusts its list price advice when rates change.

11. Apartment units

A property management company runs several apartment communities. Each row is one unit at the end of a lease, with square footage, floor, bedrooms, the previous rent, the gap to market rent for similar units, days of notice the tenant gave, an amenity score, community, unit type and season. The number target is the monthly rent for the new lease. The yes or no target is whether the unit was vacant for more than 45 days, about one unit in eight. Each row includes the unit's listing text. The federal government publishes a national rent price index, and the agent retrieves its latest value so the managers can decide how large a renewal increase to offer and when a concession costs less than a vacancy.

12. Concert venue shows

A music venue with a 1,500 seat hall books touring acts four or five nights a week. Each row is one show, with ticket price, the act's streaming following, days on sale, marketing spending, competing shows in town that night, whether there was an opening act, genre, day of week and month. The number target is ticket revenue. The yes or no target is whether the venue sold less than 60 percent of the show's capacity, about one show in seven. Each row includes the promotional blurb. Attendance changes with rain on the night and with holiday weekends, so the agent retrieves the forecast and the holiday calendar, and the venue uses them to decide whether to add marketing or release discounted tickets.

13. Grocery store weeks

A grocery chain tracks waste and empty shelves in its produce and dairy departments. Each row is one department in one store for one week, with units ordered, forecast demand, shelf life, the share of deliveries that were late, staff hours, promotion depth, department, store format and region. The number target is the week's shrink, the dollar value of product thrown away, which is small most weeks and large in a few. The yes or no target is whether the department ran out of a top item during the week, about one week in ten. Each row includes the store manager's notes. Shoppers buy differently around holidays and during heat waves, so the agent retrieves the holiday calendar and the forecast, and store managers use them to set next week's order quantities.

14. Garden center email campaign

A garden center chain sends a weekly promotional email to its loyalty members. Each row is one member who received a spring campaign, with past purchases, days since the last visit, email opens in the last 90 days, distance to the nearest store, yard size, average basket, loyalty tier, preferred category and home store. The number target is what the member spent in the two weeks after the email, which is zero for most members. The yes or no target is whether the member responded, about one in sixteen, so lift is a natural measure for this project. Each row includes the member's last chat with customer service. Gardeners shop more on fair weekends, so the agent retrieves the weekend forecast, and the marketing team uses it to decide whether to send this week's campaign and to which members.

15. Freelance marketplace projects

An online marketplace connects small businesses with freelance designers and developers around the world. Each row is one project, with budget, deadline in days, the freelancer's rating and past projects, the client's past disputes, revisions requested, project category, the freelancer's country and payment method. The number target is the final payout to the freelancer. The yes or no target is whether either side filed a dispute, about one project in fourteen. Each row includes the client's project brief. Clients pay in dollars and many freelancers receive their own currency, so the agent retrieves current exchange rates, and the marketplace uses them to set how much of a payment it holds in escrow when the client and the freelancer are in different countries.

16. Importer purchase orders

A home goods importer buys furniture and kitchenware from suppliers in Asia and Europe. Each row is one purchase order, with order value, product lines, the supplier's on time history, the quoted lead time, port of departure, shipping mode, supplier country, product category and quarter. The number target is the landed cost per unit, freight and duty included. The yes or no target is whether the order was more than a week late, about one in nine. Each row includes the supplier's messages. The dollar cost of an order depends on exchange rates, and factories close for national holidays in the supplier's country, so the agent retrieves current rates and that country's holiday calendar, and the buyers use them to decide when to order and from which supplier.

17. Retail chain new hires

A specialty retail chain hires sales associates year round and loses many in their first months. Each row is one new hire, with starting wage, prior retail experience, commute distance, scheduled hours per week, training score, the store manager's tenure, hiring channel, store type and region. The number target is sales per hour worked over the first 90 days. The yes or no target is whether the hire quit within six months, about one in six. Each row includes the hire's answer to an onboarding survey. New hires leave sooner when jobs are easy to find, so the agent retrieves the current unemployment rate, and the chain uses it to set starting wages and plan seasonal hiring.

18. Used car dealer inventory

A used car dealer buys vehicles at auction and resells them from its lots. Each row is one vehicle, with model year, mileage, the auction price paid, reconditioning cost, prior owners, accident history, make, body style and lot. The number target is the retail sale price. The yes or no target is whether the car was still unsold after 60 days on the lot, about one in eight. Each row includes its listing description. A car with an open safety recall is harder to sell, and the vehicle identification number contains a code for exactly how the manufacturer built the car, so the agent looks up recalls and decodes the number before the dealer's buyer bids.

19. Manufacturing batches

A plant makes composite panels for the building industry in batches. Each row is one batch, with resin ratio, fiber content, curing temperature, curing time, line speed, ambient humidity, operator experience, shift, resin supplier and production line. The number target is the tensile strength measured in testing. The yes or no target is whether inspectors rejected the batch, about one in twelve. Each row includes the operator's shift log. The resin cures differently in humid air, so the agent retrieves tomorrow's humidity forecast, and the plant engineers use it to set curing times for the next day's batches.

20. Farm co-op fields

An agricultural cooperative advises member farms that grow corn and soybeans across several counties. Each row is one field in one season, with acres, a soil quality index, nitrogen applied, planting date, seeding rate, growing season rainfall, days above 90 degrees, irrigation, crop, tillage method and county. The number target is yield in bushels per acre, which levels off at the top and falls sharply in drought. The yes or no target is whether the farm filed a crop insurance loss claim for the field that season, about one in ten. Each row includes the field scout's notes. The agent retrieves historical and forecast weather for the county, and the cooperative's advisers use it to recommend insurance coverage and planting dates.

DEMONSTRATION

In a regular chat, Hokie AI works from what you type and from any web search it runs. An agent begins every conversation with two things its builder put in place. The instructions state the agent's purpose and its limits. The knowledge files are documents the agent can read and quote. Hokie AI stores both with the agent, so everyone who opens the agent's link uses the same agent. With every capability switched off, the agent does not search the web, so its answers about Lumenvale come only from the instructions and the brief.

The builder writes the instructions in the five parts of SCORE. Stance is who the agent is and how it speaks. Context is what the agent knows and what it does not have yet. Objective is the decision the agent supports and what every answer must contain. Rules are the limits, especially on where the agent may take facts and numbers from. Under Examples, the builder writes one question and the answer the builder wants.

The knowledge file, the Lumenvale brief
LUMENVALE: CLASS CASE BRIEF FOR BIT 5534

Lumenvale is a made up streaming service, the class case in BIT 5534 from week 11 on. Your instructor demonstrates on Lumenvale in every class session. No team builds its project on it.

THE COMPANY

Lumenvale streams films and series to 38 million subscribers. Its eight main markets are the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, Brazil, Mexico, India and Japan. The United States accounts for 15 million of the 38 million subscribers. Lumenvale spends about 2.6 billion dollars a year on content, a little over half on originals it produces and the rest on licensed titles it rents from other studios.

The content strategy team makes three recurring decisions. The team decides which proposed titles to greenlight, and at what budget. The team decides how much to spend on marketing each title. The team also decides when to release each title, since Lumenvale can lose viewers for a title released on a crowded holiday weekend, or on a sunny weekend in its largest markets. The team judges a title by its first 28 days, because Lumenvale makes renewal and sequel decisions at day 28.

THE TITLE FILE

Your instructor releases the file lumenvale_titles.csv in class on Thursday, November 5. Until then no numbers from it are available, and this brief contains the definitions only. One row is one title, film or series, released between January 2021 and June 2026.

Columns:
title_id: Lumenvale's catalog number for the title.
title_type: Film or Series.
genre: Drama, Comedy, Thriller, Documentary, Animation, Reality, or Science Fiction and Fantasy.
origin: Original (Lumenvale produced it) or Licensed (Lumenvale rents it from another studio).
language: the original language, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Japanese, Korean or Other.
franchise: Yes if the title belongs to an existing franchise or is an adaptation of a known book, game or film, otherwise No.
budget_musd: the production budget in millions of dollars, or for a licensed title the license fee.
episodes: the number of episodes, 1 for a film.
runtime_min: the running time in minutes for a film, the average episode length for a series.
cast_recognition: a score from 0 to 100 for how well audiences in the main markets know the lead cast, measured before release.
marketing_musd: marketing spending in millions of dollars in the 28 days around release.
release_weekday: the day of the week on which Lumenvale released the title.
release_month: the month in which Lumenvale released the title.
holiday_weekend: Yes if Lumenvale released the title on a public holiday weekend in the United States, otherwise No.
critic_score: the average critic review score, from 0 to 100, published by critics in the days after release.
synopsis: the description Lumenvale showed subscribers on the title's page.
hours_28d: the number target, millions of hours subscribers watched worldwide in the first 28 days.
breakout: the yes or no target. Yes if the title was in the top ten in at least five of the eight main markets during its first 28 days. About one title in ten is a breakout.
Validation: Training or Validation, the split used in this course.

LIVE DATA

From week 13 the Lumenvale agent can retrieve two kinds of public data. With public holiday calendars for the eight main markets, the team can see which release weekends are crowded. Weekend weather forecasts for the largest cities in those markets matter because subscribers watch less on warm, clear weekends and more on cold or rainy ones. The team may move a release date on the basis of either kind of data.

HOW AN AGENT SHOULD USE THIS BRIEF

An agent may quote every fact about Lumenvale above. No other number about Lumenvale's titles is available yet. An agent gives no average, count, model result or comparison until the team supplies the title file and its own results.
