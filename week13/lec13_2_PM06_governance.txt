CORBEL HALL, GOVERNANCE SHEET
PM06. Corbel Hall is made up. With this sheet your team takes one recommendation through the five governance gates your instructor showed on Lumenvale, with one question at each gate. Your team uses the answers as material for the governance box of its progress report 2 team thread, which your team writes in its own words. About twenty five minutes, with the model run.


YOUR BUSINESS AT THE GATES

Who decides: The box office manager, who sets prices and releases discounted tickets, with the talent buyer and the marketing manager.
Who is affected: Fans.
Your live tool: get_forecast, from Open-Meteo, the forecast for Roanoke for the next 7 days. The forecast covers 7 days, so a show further out falls outside it.


THE RECOMMENDATION

Your team's recommendation for the gates is to release discounted tickets for the shows with the highest chance of selling under 60 percent. It belongs to the decision on the rescue in your brief. If your team's synthesis has a recommendation with more at stake, your team may use it instead, with the report behind it, and change the first message below to match.


RUN THE MODEL

Open the REG tool from the analytics workbench, load corbel_shows.csv, and fit this run. Then press Save report and keep the .txt file.

Target: under_60
Validation: the Validation column
Inputs: weekday, month, genre, monthly_listeners_thousands, ticket_price_usd, marketing_usd, days_on_sale, competing_shows, opening_act, holiday_weekend and forecast_rain_pct
Leave out: discount_tickets_released, because it is recorded after the outcome; promo_blurb, a text column; ticket_revenue_usd, the other target; and every other column of the file, such as the id and date columns.


THE FIRST MESSAGE

Start a new thread with your team's agent, attach the .txt report, and send the message below.

COPY:
Our recommendation is to release discounted tickets for the shows with the highest chance of selling under 60 percent. It belongs to the decision on the rescue. The attached report is a REG fit of under_60, the report the recommendation rests on. I will ask you one question about it at each of five governance gates.


GATE 1, LIVE DATA

COPY:
Call get_forecast and say which of its values bear on this recommendation, each with its date. What should the box office manager do if the tool fails?

Then open Execution Details and look at Tool Calls. Check that the agent called get_forecast and that each value in its answer matches the tool's response.


GATE 2, EVIDENCE

COPY:
Which numbers in the attached report support this recommendation, and how far can the box office manager trust them? Name the validation rows behind them and any input recorded after the outcome.


GATE 3, FAIRNESS AND PRIVACY

COPY:
Under this recommendation, prices or discounts change for some shows. Which fans are priced out, such as students at a popular act's show, and could genre stand in for the age or background of a show's audience?


GATE 4, AUTHORITY

COPY:
Which part of this recommendation is yours, and which part does the box office manager decide? Does any part go beyond the conclusions listed in our brief under CONCLUSIONS THE AGENT MAY NOT DRAW?


GATE 5, MONITORING

COPY:
If the box office manager acts on this recommendation, which number would show that it worked, which number would show that it went wrong, and when should we look again? How would we notice if get_forecast stopped working?


THEN READ THE ANSWERS

Read the five answers with the box "Reading the answers, gate by gate" on the lecture page, and decide at which gate your team has the most work to do. The main risk of your recommendation sits at that gate, and the work that would pass it is your safeguard. Write your team's governance box in your own words.
