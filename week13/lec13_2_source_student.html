Governing a Recommendation, from the Data to Monitoring
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI and your team's agent · Submission: Your reflection thread, exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas · Lecture Mode: project_lab
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Name the five governance gates a business recommendation passes on its way from the data to monitoring.
  2. Ask an agent one question at each gate, and check the agent's live tool call in Execution Details.
  3. Say what a manager should know or check before acting on a recommendation from a model and an agent.

📋 Note for Students

Today is about governance. Your instructor takes one Lumenvale recommendation through five gates, with one question for the agent at each gate. Then your team takes a recommendation from its own business through the same gates, with a governance sheet written for your business. Your team uses the answers as material for the governance box of its progress report 2 team thread.

People at your business act on a recommendation from a model and an agent. Before anyone acts on it, and after, the team takes the recommendation through five gates on its way from the data to the model report, the agent, the manager, the action and monitoring. The animation below follows one Lumenvale recommendation around that loop.

The five gates are the five questions of the governance box in your progress report 2 team thread, in the order the team reaches them.

First, live data. The value comes from a tool call with its date, and the team has a plan for the day the tool fails.

THEN evidence. Every number comes from the report's validation rows, and an estimate stays an association, with the other inputs held fixed.

THEN fairness and privacy. The team knows who the recommendation affects or leaves out, and checks whether any input stands in for a protected group.

THEN authority. The agent advises, and the person named in the brief decides.

THEN monitoring. After the manager acts, the team watches a number for success and a number for trouble, and checks that the live tool still works.

For reading
Reading the answers, gate by gate
Reading the answers, gate by gate

Live data             Your team passes the gate when each value the agent gives matches the tool's
                      response in Execution Details and carries its date. Your team has work to do when
                      the agent gives a value it did not call for, or when the team has not planned for
                      the day the tool fails.

Evidence              Your team passes the gate when the agent names the report section of every number
                      and uses the validation rows. Your team has work to do when train and validation
                      are far apart, when an input was recorded after the outcome, or when the agent
                      reads a cause into an estimate.

Fairness and privacy  Your team passes the gate when the agent names the people affected and the team
                      cannot find an input standing in for a protected group. Your team has work to do
                      when an input could stand in for one, or when acting on the recommendation means
                      using personal data people would not expect the business to use.

Authority             Your team passes the gate when the agent leaves the decision with the person named
                      in your brief. Your team has work to do when the agent decides for the manager, or
                      draws a conclusion listed in your brief under CONCLUSIONS THE AGENT MAY NOT DRAW.

Monitoring            Your team passes the gate when the agent names a number for success, a number for
                      trouble, a time to look and a person who looks. Your team has work to do when any
                      of the four is missing.

The main risk of the recommendation sits at the gate where your team has the most work to do. The
work that would pass that gate is the safeguard.
