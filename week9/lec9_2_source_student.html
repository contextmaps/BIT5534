ROC, AUC and Lift
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: exploratory
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Trace an ROC curve by lowering the threshold one row at a time and plotting TPR against FPR.
  2. Compute the AUC of a small ranking as the area under its steps, and read it as the probability that a random positive row gets a higher probability than a random negative one.
  3. Find the optimal threshold as the point on the ROC curve furthest above the diagonal, and give its FPR and TPR.
  4. Compute lift as precision divided by the share of positives, and read a lift curve against the share of rows called.
  5. Compare four models by AUC and by lift at a fixed share of rows, and explain why the two can rank them differently.
  6. Say to a classmate which model they would give a clinic with a fixed number of calls, and which number decided it.

INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

On Tuesday the threshold moved and the matrix moved with it, one threshold at a time. Today covers two tools that look at every threshold at once, the ROC curve with the area under it, and the lift curve. They answer different questions, and the clinic story from Tuesday needs both.

The file is Tuesday's diabetes_saved.csv, the same 133 test rows and the same four saved probabilities of High. The tool is Tuesday's threshold_explorer.py with two new tabs, ROC and Lift.

ROC stands for receiver operating characteristic. The name comes from radar in World War II, where a receiver operator had to call each blip on the screen an enemy plane or noise. A cautious operator raised few false alarms and missed planes, and a nervous one caught every plane and raised many false alarms. The engineers plotted the planes caught against the false alarms raised as the operator's threshold changed, and that plot is the ROC curve.

For a classifier the curve plots TPR against FPR at every threshold, from calling nobody positive to calling everybody positive. A single threshold gives one confusion matrix, and the curve gives all of them at once. AUC, the area under the ROC curve, turns the whole curve into one number between 0 and 1, which is why it is the usual measure for comparing classifiers before anybody has chosen a threshold. An AUC of 0.5 is a model that orders the rows at random.

Lift answers a budget question. A clinic can call only so many patients, a mailing can go to only so many customers, and an auditor can open only so many files. Take the top slice of the rows the model ranks highest, and lift says how many times more positives that slice holds than a random slice of the same size. A lift of 2 at 20 percent means the model's top fifth finds twice the positives a random fifth would. Marketing response models, fraud audits and outreach lists are read this way.

Section 2 of 5
DEMONSTRATION

Two doctors look at the same ten patients and each orders them from most likely sick to most likely healthy. Four of the patients are sick. The figure shows, for each place in a doctor's order, whether the patient in that place is sick. Doctor 1 put all four sick patients first, and Doctor 2 put two healthy patients among them.

Each place in the order gives one point, FPR across and TPR up, and moving the threshold past every patient traces the curve from the lower left corner to the upper right. The AUC adds up the area under the curve in vertical strips, one strip between each pair of neighboring points, and each strip's area is its average height times its width. Because each move is straight up for a sick patient or straight across for a healthy one, every strip under the doctors' curves is a rectangle, and Doctor 2's add to 0.875.

The second form gives the area its meaning. Pick one sick patient and one healthy patient at random. P(p+ > p−) is the chance the sick one gets the higher probability of being sick, so it comes first in the order. P(p+ = p−) is the chance the two get the same probability and cannot be told apart, and such a tie counts half, as if a coin settled it. The AUC is therefore the share of all sick and healthy pairs put in the right order, with each tie earning half credit. Doctor 2 has 4 times 6, 24 pairs, and puts the sick patient first in 21 of them, so the AUC is 21 over 24, 0.875. An AUC of 0.5 is a coin flip on every pair, and 1.0 is every pair in the right order.

Now lift on the same orders. Call the first i patients, and TP_i of them are sick. Calling i patients at random would find i times AP over N sick ones, 0.4 per call here. Doctor 2's first three hold two sick patients where random calls would find 1.2, a lift of 1.667.

The tool has two new tabs. The ROC tab draws the curve for the column you pick, prints its AUC, marks the optimal threshold with a green square, and puts a red point at the current threshold. The Lift tab draws lift against PP%, the share of rows at or above the threshold, with its own red point. Higher and Lower move both points, and the values at the point are printed above each plot.
