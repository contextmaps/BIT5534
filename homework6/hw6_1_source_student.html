Neural Networks on Delivery Minutes, in the City and in a New Town
Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Set up a neural network fit on a business file, with a target, inputs, a five fold validation column, a network and a number of starts, and read the Fit, Network, Starts and Validation column tabs.
  2. Compare one identity neuron, which is linear regression, with networks of tanh neurons by train and validation R squared and by the spread across the folds.
  3. Remove one input on purpose and explain why a straight line and a network react to the removal differently.
  4. Fit networks of three sizes, three numbers of neurons, on a small file and recognize overfitting in the train and validation figures.
  5. Compare a one third holdout drawn twice with five folds on a small file, and say which figure belongs in a report.
  6. Recommend a model for a business decision from ranges of results, and defend the recommendation against a challenge.

Hokie Eats is a food delivery platform in Blacksburg, made up for this homework. Every row in the Hokie Eats files is one delivered order. Minutes is the time from the order to the customer's door. Hour is the time of day the order was placed, so 18.5 means 6:30 in the evening. Weekend and Rain are 1 or 0. Distance_km is the trip from the restaurant to the door, Items is the number of items in the order, Orders_in_Queue is the number of orders the restaurant's kitchen already had when the order came in, and Courier_Months is how long the courier has worked for Hokie Eats. Late is 1 when Minutes went over the 30 minutes promised at checkout.

An order's minutes add up from three places. The kitchen has to cook the order, and a kitchen with many orders already waiting, or an order with many items, takes longer. The road takes the courier from the restaurant to the door, and the trip depends on the distance, on the traffic at that time of day and day of the week, and on the weather. The courier matters too, because an experienced courier knows the restaurants and the streets. All seven input columns are known the moment the customer presses Order.

Hokie Eats wants a model that predicts Minutes from what is known when the customer presses Order, so that the promise at checkout is honest. Part 1 predicts Minutes, a number. Part 2 predicts Late, a category, the way Thursday's lecture turned Price into MedPrice. Late is built from Minutes, so in part 1 Late stays out of the inputs.

Two models from earlier in the semester live inside nn_explorer.py. One identity neuron passes the weighted sum of the inputs through unchanged. With a number target, one identity neuron is linear regression. With a category target, the same single identity neuron is logistic regression, because the tool turns the straight line score into a probability for each class. So the tool can fit the regression you already know and a network on the same folds, and the comparison is fair.

Case one is the city file, 1,000 orders. You set up the fits yourself, step by step, and compare a straight line with two networks. Then you take one column away and watch which model notices.

Case two is a new town where Hokie Eats opened three weeks ago and has delivered 150 orders. The same settings meet a much smaller file, and you decide what the new town should launch with.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Set up the first fit in five steps. Every later fit in this case changes one thing from this setup.

Step 1, the file. Open nn_explorer.py, press Open file and choose delivery_orders.csv. The Target column reads Minutes and the type reads number. Every numeric column starts selected in Input columns. Click Late to unselect Late, because Late is built from Minutes. Seven inputs stay selected.

Step 2, the validation column. In the Validation column box choose k folds, leave k at 5, type 123 in Seed and press Make the column. Use the column now reads fold5. Every row belongs to one of five folds, and each of the five fits trains on four folds and is judged on the fifth.

Step 3, the network. In the Hidden layers box, set tanh in the First row to 0 and identity to 1. Leave the Second row at 0. One identity neuron passes the weighted sum through unchanged, so the whole network is a straight line in the seven inputs, which is linear regression.

Step 4, the fitting. Leave Standardize inputs ticked, set Number of starts to 10 and the Seed under the starts to 123.

Step 5, press Fit the network.

Check three things before you read anything else. The Validation column tab should list five folds of 200 rows. The Fit tab should say Parameters: 1 x (7 + 1) + 1 x (1 + 1) = 10. The last line of the Fit tab should read Average validation R squared over 5 folds: 0.663. If all three agree, your setup is the setup this page is written about.

The Fit tab holds a train line and a validation line for each fold, then the average validation R squared. The Network tab draws the network with the parameter count. The Actual vs predicted tab plots every order's predicted Minutes against the real Minutes. The Starts tab lists all ten starts in every fold, with the train and validation error of each, and the tool keeps the start with the smallest validation error. The Validation column tab shows which rows sit in which fold.

Now fit two networks. Set identity back to 0 and tanh to 3, and press Fit the network. Then set tanh to 10 and fit again. Each fit of ten tanh neurons takes a little while, because ten starts run in each of five folds. Write down three things for every fit, the parameter count, the average train and validation R squared, and the lowest and highest validation R squared of the five folds.

Then take one column away. Click Hour to unselect Hour, keep everything else, and fit one identity neuron, then ten tanh neurons. Click Hour again afterwards, so Hour is back for case two.

Four moves, in order. The card below carries the four moves.

Move one reports the three fits AND move two works through the Hour result AND move three reads the Starts tab AND move four works through what Hokie AI disputes. All four.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you fit these networks yourself in a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested and not repeated back to you. Before the numbers, write in two or three sentences how you expect the kitchen, road and courier columns to move Minutes, so Hokie AI can hold your expectations against the fits. Then report the three fits with Hour in, one identity neuron, three tanh and ten tanh, each with the parameter count, the average train and validation R squared and the range of the five validation folds. Say which size, meaning which number of neurons, you would choose, and use the ranges to say whether two sizes really differ.

Move two is the reason for the case. Report the two fits without Hour beside the same two fits with Hour. One model barely noticed that Hour was gone, and the other lost a great deal. Write your explanation of why, then ask Hokie AI to test the explanation. Think about the times of day when a delivery is slow, and what a straight line in Hour can say about the day.

Move three opens the Starts tab for the fit of ten tanh neurons with Hour in. Pick one fold and report the validation MSE of the kept start and of the worst start. Say what the gap means for anybody who fits a network once and reports the result.

Move four is where the exchange earns its place. Where Hokie AI says you are reading too much into a number, ask what a correctly stated version would be. Where you think Hokie AI is wrong, say so and give Hokie AI the number from your tool that settles the question. You can see the fits and Hokie AI cannot, so a disagreement is yours to settle.

One move is not one message. Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Hokie Eats opened in a new town three weeks ago and has delivered 150 orders there. The operations manager wants a model for the new town's promise at checkout, and new_town_orders.csv holds every order so far. The town is new, but the orders behave like the city's. Only the amount of data is different.

Open new_town_orders.csv in nn_explorer.py. Click Late to unselect Late, as in case one, and keep the other seven inputs.

First, a one third holdout, drawn twice. In the Validation column box choose holdout, leave Validation share at 0.3333 and Test share at 0, type 123 in Seed and press Make the column. Fit three tanh neurons with ten starts and starts seed 123, and write down the train and validation R squared. Then type 7 in the Seed of the Validation column box, press Make the column again, which replaces the holdout column, and fit the same three tanh neurons. Write down the train and validation R squared again.

Then five folds. Choose k folds, leave k at 5, type 123 in Seed and press Make the column. Fit one identity neuron, then three tanh, then ten tanh, ten starts each, and write down the parameter count, the train and validation averages and the fold range for each fit.

Check three things. The first holdout column holds 100 train rows and 50 validation rows. The five fold column holds five folds of 30 rows. One identity neuron on five folds gives Average validation R squared over 5 folds: 0.628.

Keep your case one numbers beside you, from your thread or from notes. The comparison is half of the case.

Four moves, in order. The card below carries the four moves.

Move one frames the decision and reports the holdout pair AND move two reports the three sizes on five folds AND move three compares the new town with the city AND move four makes a recommendation and defends the recommendation. All four.

Move one opens the block. Write the operations manager's question in your own words. Then report the two holdout fits of three tanh neurons, seed 123 and seed 7, and say how far the validation R squared moved between the two draws. Ask Hokie AI what a one third holdout on 150 orders can and cannot tell the manager.

Move two reports the five fold fits, one identity neuron, three tanh and ten tanh, each with the parameter count, the train and validation averages and the fold range. Name the fit that overfits and give the numbers that show the overfitting. Use the ranges to say which fits you can tell apart.

Move three sets the new town beside the city. Put the same three fits from case one next to the new town fits and say what changed and why. The settings did not change. Count how many train rows each fit learns from in the new town and how many parameters ten tanh neurons hold, and say what the count has to do with the result.

Move four recommends and defends. Write what the new town should launch with, the straight line, a small network, or something else such as waiting for more orders, and the numbers the recommendation rests on. Ask Hokie AI for the strongest case against the recommendation, then answer it. Change the recommendation if the case is good, and say why if the case is not.
