Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Fit a neural network to a category target and read the misclassification rate and the confusion table.
  2. Compare one identity neuron, which is logistic regression fitted inside the network tool, with networks of tanh neurons, by misclassification and by the kind of error each model makes.
  3. Change one setting at a time, the size, the activation, the layers and the starts, and read the results as ranges.
  4. Put a cost on each kind of error and compare models by what the errors cost the business.
  5. Recognize target leakage when a column known only after the outcome makes a model nearly perfect.
  6. Decide which columns a model may use by when each column becomes known, and defend the decision against a challenge.

Part 2 stays with Hokie Eats, the made up delivery platform from part 1. The target is now Late, 1 when an order took more than the 30 minutes promised at checkout and 0 when the order arrived on time. In the city file 219 of the 1,000 orders were late. Late is a category, so the tool reports misclassification, the share of orders the model puts in the wrong class, and a confusion table, the way Thursday's lecture did for MedPrice. Late is built from Minutes, so in part 2 Minutes stays out of the inputs.

The seven inputs are the same as in part 1. Orders_in_Queue and Items describe the kitchen, Distance_km, Hour, Weekend and Rain describe the road, and Courier_Months describes the courier, and all seven are known the moment the customer presses Order.

One identity neuron with a category target is logistic regression, the model from earlier in the semester. The single neuron forms a straight line score from the seven inputs, and the tool turns the score into a probability for each class, which is what logistic regression does. In part 1 the same single neuron with a number target was linear regression. So nn_explorer.py fits logistic regression and the networks on the same folds, and the comparison is fair.

Hokie Eats wants to flag an order at checkout when the order is likely to be late, so that the app can show a longer promise. A flag can be wrong in two ways, and the two ways cost different amounts. When the model says on time and the order arrives late, Hokie Eats gives the customer a 5 dollar credit for the broken promise. When the model says late and the order would have arrived on time, the app shows a promise ten minutes longer, and a longer promise loses about one order in five to a competitor, at about 15 dollars of margin per lost order, which is 3 dollars per false flag on average.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Set up the first fit in five steps, as in part 1, with one difference in the target.

Step 1, the file and the target. Open nn_explorer.py, press Open file and choose delivery_orders.csv. Choose Late in Target column. The type changes to category on its own, because Late holds only 0 and 1. In Input columns, click Minutes to unselect Minutes. Seven inputs stay selected.

Step 2, the validation column. Choose k folds, leave k at 5, type 123 in Seed and press Make the column.

Step 3, the network. In the First row set tanh to 0 and identity to 1. With a category target, one identity neuron is logistic regression, a straight line in the seven inputs turned into a probability of each class.

Step 4, the fitting. Leave Standardize inputs ticked, set Number of starts to 10 and the Seed under the starts to 123.

Step 5, press Fit the network.

Check three things. The Fit tab should say Parameters: 1 x (7 + 1) + 2 x (1 + 1) = 12, two outputs because Late has two classes. The Fit tab should read Average validation misclassification over 5 folds: 0.126. In the confusion table of validation rows, actual 1 should hold 86 predicted 0 and 133 predicted 1. If all three agree, read on.

The confusion table reads with the actual class down the side and the predicted class across the top. With five folds, every order is predicted once by the fold model that did not learn from the order, so the table covers all 1,000 orders. The cell in row 1 and column 0 counts late orders the model called on time, the 5 dollar error. The cell in row 0 and column 1 counts on time orders the model flagged as late, the 3 dollar error.

Now the settings grid from Thursday's lecture, four settings that shape a network. Size is the number of neurons, how big the network is. Activation is the type of neuron, tanh, logistic, relu or identity. Layers is one hidden layer or two. Starts is the number of random starting points the tool tries, keeping the best on validation.

The base setting is three tanh neurons in one layer with ten starts. Fit the base, then change one setting at a time from the base and fit again, four times.

Size, ten tanh in place of three. Activation, three relu in place of three tanh. Layers, three tanh in the First row and three tanh in the Second row. Starts, the base with Number of starts set to 1.

Write down five things for every fit, the parameter count, the average train and validation misclassification, the lowest and highest validation fold, and the two error cells of the confusion table.

Four moves, in order. The card below carries the four moves.

Move one reports the straight line and the base AND move two reads the grid AND move three prices the errors AND move four works through what Hokie AI disputes. All four.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you fit these models yourself in a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested and not repeated back to you. Then report logistic regression, the one identity neuron, and the base, three tanh, each with the parameter count, the train and validation misclassification, the fold range and the two error cells. Say which kind of error the base reduced most.

Move two reads the grid. Report the four changes. For each change, say whether the change helped, hurt or cannot be told apart from the base, using the fold ranges and not the averages alone. Name any fit where train and validation pull apart, with the numbers.

Move three prices the errors. For logistic regression and for the setting you would choose, multiply the late orders called on time by 5 dollars and the on time orders flagged as late by 3 dollars, and add. Report both totals for the 1,000 orders and the difference. Ask Hokie AI to check the arithmetic and to test whether the cheaper model is the better choice.

Move four is where the exchange earns its place. Where Hokie AI says you are reading too much into a number, ask what a correctly stated version would be. Where you think Hokie AI is wrong, say so and give Hokie AI the number from your tool that settles the question.
