Logistic Regression with Multiple Inputs
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: consolidating
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Write the logistic model for many inputs at once and say what the fitted equation produces at each step, from the linear combination to the odds to the probability.
  2. State what a coefficient is, that it carries a standard error, a p-value and a logworth, and that its exponent is the odds multiplier for a one unit increase.
  3. Read a whole-model likelihood ratio chi-square and say where its degrees of freedom came from.
  4. Explain that a categorical input contributes one column per level, so that individual levels can carry their own p-values.
  5. Read a two by two table of actual against predicted and say what each row percentage measures.
Section 1 of 5
INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

Tuesday's model had one input. The response was still zero or one, the sigmoid still turned a number into a probability, and the exponentiated slope was still the multiplier on the odds for a one unit increase.

Today the same machine takes many inputs at once, and almost nothing about it changes. What changes is what you have to read. A model with one input has one estimate to judge. The model you will fit today has sixty-five, and the work of the hour is deciding which of them you would repeat out loud to somebody who has to act on it.

One equation, one table of terms, one whole-model test, one figure about categorical inputs, and one two by two. Everything with a formula in it is in this section.

The intercept and the coefficients are chosen by maximum likelihood, which picks the values that make the responses you actually observed as likely as the model can make them. You will not compute it by hand today or any other day.

💬 Discussion

Two questions before we look at a fitted model:

  • Fifty levels of one column cost fifty parameters in the whole-model test. What would you want back in return?
  • A row percentage on the top row and a row percentage on the bottom row answer different business questions. Which one costs money when it is wrong?
Section 2 of 5
DEMONSTRATION

One model, fitted to a real file with 3,333 customers and fifteen candidate inputs. This is the same file you run in section 4, so every number here is a number your own tool has to reproduce.

The whole model returns a likelihood ratio chi-square of 686.5406 on 65 degrees of freedom, which is where the state column went. Fifty levels contribute fifty parameters AND the four remaining categorical levels contribute four more AND the eleven numeric inputs contribute eleven.

💬 Discussion

Carry these into your own run:

  • The whole-model p-value says the model is not nothing. The top row of the table says what it is worth. Why do those two answers feel so different?
  • International plan carries a logworth of 45.50 and Virginia carries 1.72. What would you do differently on the strength of each?
