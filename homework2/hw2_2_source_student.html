Logistic Regression with Many Inputs and What the Report Leaves Out
Platform: Hokie AI (build) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Fit a logistic regression with numeric and categorical inputs together, and account for the number of degrees of freedom the model reports against the number of input columns they selected.
  2. Separate the terms whose coefficients this sample can tell apart from zero from the terms it cannot, and say what the whole model still establishes.
  3. Read a classification table, say what the 0.5 cutoff is, and say what would have to be true before a lender moved it.
  4. Say what an omitted level is under effect coding, and why an omitted level carries an estimate of its own.
  5. Say what an effect test reports about one input column, and why a column of per-level p-values does not answer the same question.
  6. Specify an update to an existing script in their own words, and close its gaps through an interview they asked for.
  7. Run the updated script, compare an effect test against the contingency test they ran in part 1, and reach a recommendation a lender could act on.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 60 points, 15 for the first M block, 30 for the second M block and 15 for the reflection in the R block.

Case one read the report that multi_logistic.py printed. Case two is about what that report leaves out, and about changing the script so that it stops leaving it out. You will find the gaps in the first M block and specify the repair in the second. Work in the same thread.

Open the first M block and go looking before you specify anything. Twelve turns is the ceiling and no script comes out of this M block.

Two places in your case one report are worth opening. The page names both, because finding them is not the work. Understanding them is.

The first place is the line near the top of the report headed Omitted categorical levels. It names Part-time for Employment_Status, Rent for Housing_Status and Auto for Loan_Purpose. Now look down the term table and count the rows for each of those three columns. Employment_Status has three rows and four levels. Housing_Status has two rows and three levels. Loan_Purpose has three rows and four levels. Every one of the three levels named at the top of the report has no row anywhere in the table, so the report tells you that those three levels exist and then reports nothing about any of them.

The second place is Loan_Purpose itself. It has four levels and the table prints a row for three of them, Debt consolidation at a p-value of 0.2342, Home improvement at 0.6439 and Medical at 0.1944. The fourth level, Auto, is the one the script omitted and it carries no p-value anywhere. A lender asks whether loan purpose belongs on the application form at all. A p-value attached to one level answers a question about that level, so no number in your report answers the lender.

By the end of the first M block you should be able to state the following four points in your own words.

First, what effect coding does to a column of categories, why a column of four levels produces three columns of numbers, and what the numbers in those columns mean.

Second, why the omitted level carries an estimate of its own, how that estimate is related to the estimates of the printed levels in the same column, and whether a standard error and a p-value can be obtained for it.

Third, what an effect test is, what question it answers about one whole input column, how many degrees of freedom it carries, and why a column of per-level p-values does not answer the same question.

Fourth, what a lender would do with an effect test that a column of per-level p-values leaves them unable to do.

Do not spend one turn on each of the four points. A single well-built opening question covers the first point and the second point together. Spend the turns you save on the third point and the fourth point, which are the two points case two is built on.

Ask Hokie AI whether the omitted level can carry a standard error and a p-value, and press if the answer is vague, because the second M block asks you to specify a script that prints them.
