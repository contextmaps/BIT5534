Regression on Satellite Readings and on Their Principal Components
Platform: Hokie AI (build) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Assemble a regression file from a response column and a set of input columns, with the response in the first column.
  2. Run a multiple regression, report the R squared and the adjusted R squared, and read a logworth plot for the inputs that reach significance.
  3. Reduce a model to its significant inputs, run it again, and say what each of the two numbers did.
  4. Check a summary an assistant gives back against the paragraph it was built from, and correct what it changed, dropped or added.
  5. Say whether the inputs a regression keeps resemble the components a principal component analysis retains, and say why a count of significant columns and a count of retained components are not the same quantity.
  6. Name a group of correlated readings where one member survived a reduction and another was dropped, and say what that tells you about those columns.
  7. Run the same response against the principal components of the same inputs, and compare the two full models.
  8. Say why a model on all the components returns what a model on all the original columns returns.
  9. Reduce the component model by the eigenvalue rule and compare it against the reduced original model.
  10. Read the variance inflation factor column of a regression report and say what it says about the inputs of that model.
  11. Weigh three properties of a component model, which are inputs that are uncorrelated with each other, a reduction small enough to plot, and a reduction computed without seeing the response, against the named inputs of a reading model.
  12. Choose between two reduced models and defend the choice on something other than fit.

The count of those rows is k, and on this data it is three. Keep Signal_Strength and the first k score columns, starting at PC1, delete the rest, and save. Run the script on that file and write down both numbers. The run writes the folder pca_regression_reduced_outputs.

One more reading before you open the block. Open report.txt from the original_regression_reduced_outputs folder and report.txt from the pca_regression_reduced_outputs folder, and find the column headed variance_inflation_factor in the TERMS table of each. In the reading model those values run from 1.5 to 3.3. In the component model every value is 1.00. Write both down, because move four uses them.

Before the moves, three properties of the reduced component model are worth knowing, because move four puts them to Hokie AI and move five weighs them against what the reading model offers.

The first property is that the score columns are uncorrelated with each other. The word for that in class was orthogonal, and zero correlation is what orthogonal means for these columns. The variance inflation factor of 1.00 you just read for every component is the evidence on your own screen. Each coefficient in the component model carries the whole of its own direction, and no coefficient moves when another component is added or dropped. The eight readings in original_regression_reduced.csv sit at 1.5 to 3.3 instead, so those coefficients are sharing credit with each other.

The second property is that three columns can be looked at. Three components carry 78.30 percent of the variance of twelve readings, which is the cumulative percent in row three of your eigenvalues.csv, and the three planes you set in pca_explorer.py in part 1, which are PC1 with PC2, PC1 with PC3 and PC2 with PC3, are three faces of one three dimensional picture. You already plotted two of those score columns in Excel in part 1, and the third component is one more axis. Eight readings have no picture at all.

The third property is that nothing in the analysis saw Signal_Strength. pca_explorer.py ran on the twelve readings alone in part 1, and the three directions it returned are where the response turned out to live. The other side of that is in your own twelve component model, where the logworth.png in pca_regression_outputs puts PC4, PC6, PC7 and PC9 past the dashed line while each of those four carries under four percent of the variance. Ordering components by variance is not the same as ordering them by what they say about the response.

Now open the M block and put five things to Hokie AI, in order. The card below carries them.

Move one is a paragraph you write. Say what the dataset is, that the inputs this time are the principal components of the same twelve readings, give the R squared and the adjusted R squared of the twelve component model, say how many components the eigenvalue rule retained and give the R squared and the adjusted R squared of the reduced component model AND move two asks how the two full models compare, the one on the twelve readings and the one on the twelve components AND move three asks what is interesting about that comparison and makes Hokie AI give the reason AND move four names the three properties of the reduced component model that the page listed above, which are the zero correlation between the score columns, the three columns that can be plotted, and the reduction computed without the response, and asks what each one is worth on this data AND move five compares the two reduced models, asks which one is better to use and asks why. All five.

Move two is a comparison you can make from numbers you already hold, which are the R squared and the adjusted R squared of the twelve reading model and of the twelve component model. Put it to Hokie AI as a question about those four numbers and not as a request for a lecture on principal components.

Move three is the one to spend time on. There is a reason two models built from different columns return the same fit, and it is a fact about what the components are, not a coincidence. Make it give the reason and make sure the reason accounts for the adjusted R squared as well.

Move four is one message carrying all three properties. Ask what the zero correlation between the score columns is worth to somebody reading the coefficients, and put the variance inflation factors you read from the two reduced models in the same message AND ask what being able to plot three columns is worth when twelve readings cannot be plotted AND ask what it means that the analysis found those three directions without ever seeing Signal_Strength. All three. Then take at least one of the three answers a step further with a question aimed at something that answer said. Asking a second general question about principal components does not count as taking an answer further.

Move five is the task. One reduced model holds the eight readings that cleared significance and the other holds the three components the eigenvalue rule retained, and they do not return the same fit. A larger fit is one argument, the three properties of move four are arguments on the other side, and neither set settles it. Ask what each model costs the person who has to decide which satellite to act on, then say which model you would use.
