Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Fit a support vector machine for a category target and for a number target with svm_explorer.py on a validation column the file supplies.
  2. Read on the two input plot how the cost C moves the margin and the number of support vectors, and how gamma moves the smoothness of the boundary.
  3. Recognize overfitting in train, validation and test figures as C and gamma rise.
  4. Use the grid of C and gamma to choose a setting by its validation figure, and say what the test figure adds.
  5. Turn a regression error into a business figure and recommend a pricing model with its limits.

Case two is cabin_rates.csv from Bramblewood Cabin Rentals, a cabin rental company in the mountains, made up for this homework. Each row is one booked night, with the nightly rate the guest paid and eight things known when the booking was made. You fit a straight line and a support vector machine to the nightly rate, search C and gamma, and turn the result into dollars.

Both files carry their own Validation column, with every row marked Training, Validation or Test. Use that column in every fit, so your figures and your classmates' figures come from the same rows. A support vector machine has no random start, so with the same column and the same settings every machine gives the same numbers, and the checks on this page are exact.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Set up the first fit in three steps. Every later fit in this case changes C, gamma or the kernel and nothing else.

Step 1, the file. Open svm_explorer.py, press Open file and choose spirals.csv. Set the Target column to Arm, and the type reads category. X1 and X2 are selected in Input columns.

Step 2, the validation column. Use the column already reads Validation, the file's own column. Leave it there, and press nothing in the rest of the Validation column box.

Step 3, the first fit. Choose the linear kernel, type 1 in Cost C, leave Standardize inputs ticked and press Fit the model.

Check three things before you read anything else. The Validation column tab lists 300 Training, 100 Validation and 100 Test rows. The Fit tab gives train misclassification 0.337 and validation 0.320. The Fit tab says 214 of the 300 train rows are support vectors. If all three agree, your setup is the setup this page is written about.

Now the fits. Open the Two input plot tab after every one of them, because the plot is half of this case. The plot shades the region each arm is predicted in, draws the boundary as a solid line and the margin as dashed lines, and circles every support vector.

First the kernel. Switch to the RBF kernel, keep C 1, leave Gamma empty and fit. The tool uses 1 divided by the number of inputs, 0.5, and says so.

Then C, with gamma held at 1. Type 1 in Gamma, fit with C 0.1, then with C 1000. Watch the dashed margin lines and the circled points.

Then gamma, with C held at 10. Fit with gamma 0.01, then 1, then 100. Watch how smooth the boundary is.

Last, the grid. Leave the default lists in the Grid of C and gamma box, press Search the grid, and read the two heat maps on the Grid tab. Fit the pair the grid outlines and read the test row on the Fit tab.

For every fit, write down the train, validation and test misclassification and the number of support vectors.

Four moves, in order. The card below carries the four moves.

Move one reports the kernel AND move two reads C on the plot AND move three reads gamma on the plot AND move four reads the grid and defends a choice. All four.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you fit these models yourself in a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested. Report the linear fit and the RBF fit with their figures, and describe in a sentence or two what each boundary looks like on the plot. Say why a straight line cannot do better on two spirals.

Move two holds gamma at 1 and compares C 0.1 with C 1000. Report both fits with their support vector counts, and describe what happened to the dashed margin lines and to the circled points between the two. Explain why a cheap slack, a small C, makes so many points support vectors.

Move three holds C at 10 and compares gamma 0.01, 1 and 100. Report the three fits and describe the boundary each draws. Name the setting that overfits and give the train, validation and test figures that show it.

Move four reads the grid. Report the pair the grid outlined, its train and validation figures, and the test figure you read after fitting it. The validation rows chose the pair, so ask Hokie AI what the test figure tells you that the validation figure cannot. Then say which setting you would trust on a new set of points, and defend the choice against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

One move is not one message. Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Bramblewood Cabin Rentals sets each night's rate by hand, and the owner wants a model to suggest a rate when a booking comes in. The rate depends on the cabin and on the booking. Bedrooms counts the bedrooms, Lake_km is the distance to the lake, View is Lake, Mountain or No View, and Hot_Tub is Yes or No. Season is the season of the stay, Weekend is 1 for a Friday or Saturday night, Rating is the cabin's guest rating out of 5, and Days_Ahead is how many days before the stay the guest booked.

Open cabin_rates.csv in svm_explorer.py. The Target column reads Nightly_Rate and the type reads number. The numeric inputs start selected. Click View, Hot_Tub and Season to add them, so all eight inputs are selected. The tool turns each text column into one 0 or 1 column per level after the first, so the model sees 11 inputs. Use the column reads Validation.

Leave Epsilon at 0.1 for every fit in this case. The cost C, gamma and the kernel are the settings you change.

Check three things. The Validation column tab lists 540 Training, 180 Validation and 180 Test rows. The Fit tab's Inputs line lists 11 inputs. The linear kernel with C 1 gives validation R squared 0.800 and validation RMSE 34.6.

Now the fits. The linear kernel with C 1 is your straight line. Then the RBF kernel with C 1 and Gamma empty, which the tool sets to 1 divided by 11 inputs. Then push both settings, C 1000 with gamma 1. Then the grid with the default lists, and the pair the grid outlines, with its test row. For every fit, write down the train, validation and test R squared, the validation RMSE, which is in dollars, and the support vectors.

Four moves, in order. The card below carries the four moves.

Move one sets the business question AND move two compares the straight line with the default RBF AND move three pushes C and gamma and reads the grid AND move four turns the error into dollars and makes a recommendation. All four.

Move one opens the block. Write the owner's question in your own words. Before any number, write which of the eight inputs you expect to raise the rate and whether you expect any of them to work differently in summer and in winter, so Hokie AI can hold your expectations against the fits.

Move two reports the linear kernel and the default RBF, with train and validation R squared. Say which one has the edge on the validation rows, and what the train figure of the RBF fit warns you about.

Move three reports C 1000 with gamma 1 and says what happened. Then report the grid's outlined pair with its train, validation and test figures, and say what the two heat maps show about where the good settings sit.

Move four turns the RMSE into a business statement. Validation RMSE is the typical size of a wrong suggestion, in dollars a night. Compare the straight line with the grid's pair in those terms, recommend which the owner should use, and ask Hokie AI for the strongest case against your recommendation. Remember that a straight line can be written down as one coefficient for each input, and an RBF machine cannot. Answer the case, and change your recommendation if the case is good.

One move is not one message. Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.
