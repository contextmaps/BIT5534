Predicting Guest Credits and Return Stays from Feedback Topics
Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Save NMF topic weights and a BERTopic topic column from text_explorer.py and use each as inputs to a regression in reg_explorer.py.
  2. Compare the validation fit of the two inputs for a number target, and read the largest logworth term with its estimate.
  3. Explain from a comment's own words why one input predicts its credit closer than the other.
  4. Compare the two inputs for a category target, and explain from the topic tables why the result reverses.
  5. Recommend which text input a hotel should use for which target, and say what each one cannot see.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

First make the inputs. Open text_explorer.py and open guest_feedback.csv from its window. Set Text column to comment, Topics k to 10, LSA components to 5, Keep terms in at least this many documents to 2, and Minimum topic size to 10. It opens at 15, so change it. The Embeddings box names guest_feedback_embeddings.csv with Match rows by comment_id. If it says No embeddings file, press Choose file and pick guest_feedback_embeddings.csv. Press Run the analysis. The line under the page title reads 400 documents, 1041 terms, DTM 400 x 1041 with 7799 nonzero cells (1.87 percent full). Embeddings matched for every document, then a topic count that may differ from the reference run's 10.

Under Features for the week 8 tools, keep Topic weights and BERTopic topic ticked, clear LSA scores, Number of terms and BERTopic scores, and press Save file with them. Keep the name it offers, guest_feedback_features.csv, in your hw9_2 folder. The status line names 11 new columns, TOPIC_1 to TOPIC_10 and BT_topic.

Now fit the first model. Open reg_explorer.py and open guest_feedback_features.csv. Set Target column to credit_dollars, which the tool fits as a number. In Input columns, nights and TOPIC_1 to TOPIC_10 are selected for you, so click nights to clear it. Validation column shows Use the column Validation. Press Fit the model.

Check three things on the Fit tab. The validation row reads 120 rows and R squared 0.630. The train row reads 280 rows and R squared 0.607. In the terms table, TOPIC_4 has the largest logworth, about 28.6, with an estimate of about 956. A newer or older scikit-learn can move the last decimal, nothing more.

Save this model's predictions. Under Save for stacking, press Save predictions, type the name guest_feedback_topic_pred.csv, and save it in your hw9_2 folder. The file gets a column named REG pred credit_dollars.

Then fit the second model. In Input columns, click the ten TOPIC names to clear them, click BT_topic, and press Fit the model. The tool turns BT_topic into one column per topic after the first, so each estimate is in dollars against a comment in the first topic in the BERTopic table, BT0 or BT00. On the BERTopic tab of the text_explorer page, write down that first topic's top words. Then write down the validation and train R squared, and the term with the largest logworth with its estimate. On the reference run the validation R squared is 0.665 and the largest logworth belongs to the topic whose top words are bugs, red, bed and tiny, BT3 there, at about 37 with an estimate near 256 against BT0. On other runs the R squared lies between about 0.66 and 0.67, the bed bug topic may carry another number, and its logworth and estimate change with the first topic, so find it by its words and report your own figures. Save these predictions too, under the name guest_feedback_bt_pred.csv.

Last, two comments from the Validation rows, GF20282 and GF20195. GF20282 never says bug or bed, and GF20195 never says charge or bill. Open guest_feedback_topic_pred.csv and guest_feedback_bt_pred.csv in Excel or any spreadsheet, find each comment by its comment_id, and write down credit_dollars and REG pred credit_dollars from each file. Then open guest_feedback_features.csv, find the same two rows, and write down the largest of the ten TOPIC weights in each row with its column name, and the BT_topic. Read the two comments in full on the text_explorer page. Use Find on the Map tab.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one predicts before you fit AND move two reads the TOPIC fit AND move three reads the BT_topic fit AND move four explains the two comments. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you read tools Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what they show, and that you want each reading tested. Before any figure, say which kinds of comment you expect to earn a large credit, and which input you expect to predict credit_dollars better on comments written in unusual words, ten NMF weights from tf-idf or one BERTopic topic from the embeddings, and why. Ask Hokie AI to argue against your expectation.

Move two reports the TOPIC fit, the validation and train R squared, and the term with the largest logworth with its estimate. Name that topic's top words from the Topics tab of the text_explorer page, and say what the estimate means for a comment whose weight on that topic is 0.1 higher.

Move three reports the BT_topic fit, the same figures, the topic with the largest logworth by its words, and the words of the first topic it is measured against. Say which input fits better on the Validation rows and by how much, and say what the BT_topic estimate means in dollars.

Move four quotes GF20282 and GF20195 and reports, for each, the actual credit, both predictions, the largest TOPIC weight with its column, and the BT_topic. Say which prediction is closer for each comment. For GF20282, explain from its words why its TOPIC weight is weak while BERTopic placed it with the bed bug complaints. For GF20195, the TOPIC weight and the BT_topic both point to billing, so explain why both predictions still sit below its actual credit. Defend the explanations against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Case one predicted a number. Case two predicts a category, whether the guest booked again, from the same two inputs. In reg_explorer.py, keep guest_feedback_features.csv open and set Target column to booked_again, which the tool fits as a category by logistic regression. Set Positive class to Yes. It opens at No, the less frequent level, so change it.

In Input columns, nights, credit_dollars and TOPIC_1 to TOPIC_10 are selected for you. Click nights and credit_dollars to clear them, and press Fit the model.

Check two things on the Fit tab. The validation row reads 120 rows, misclassification 0.358 and RSquare (U) 0.078. In the terms table, TOPIC_1 has the largest logworth, 2.45, with an estimate of about 17.3.

Then click the ten TOPIC names to clear them, click BT_topic, and fit again. Write down the validation misclassification and RSquare (U), and the term with the largest logworth with its estimate and its topic's words. On the reference run RSquare (U) is 0.036, misclassification 0.392, and the largest logworth, about 3.1, belongs to the bed bug topic with an estimate near minus 1.9, a lower chance of booking again than a comment in BT0. On other runs RSquare (U) lies between about 0.026 and 0.045, and the largest logworth may belong to the outlier row instead, with a positive estimate. Report your own figures.

The two fits reverse case one, and the topic tables on the text_explorer page show why. On the Topics tab, write down T1's top words and, in the table below with Column set to department, T1's row of counts. On the BERTopic tab, set Column to department and find where the 66 Guest relations comments went. Write down the topic or the outlier row that holds most of them, its top words, and its row of counts. If one topic holds mostly Guest relations comments, write down how many it holds and where the rest of the 66 went. To read the comments inside a topic, open the Map tab, set Color by to topic (NMF) or topic (BERTopic), double click that topic in the legend so it shows alone, and hover over its points. Guest relations is the praise, so these rows tell you where each method put the happiest guests.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one predicts before you fit AND move two reads the TOPIC fit and T1 AND move three reads the BT_topic fit and where the praise went AND move four recommends an input for each target. All four moves.

Move two reports the TOPIC fit, misclassification and RSquare (U) on the Validation rows, and TOPIC_1 with its estimate and its sign. Report T1's top words and its department row, read its comments on the Map tab, and say what T1 is built from. On Tuesday you saw that not, no and never are stop words, and the comments in T1 say things like not a single bug in the bed.

Move three reports the BT_topic fit and its largest logworth topic by its words. Report where the Guest relations comments went in BERTopic, and what else sits in that topic or in the outlier row. Say which input fits booked_again better on the Validation rows and by how much, and why, from T1's department row and the BERTopic rows that hold the praise.

Move four recommends one text input for credit_dollars and one for booked_again, and says what each input cannot see that matters for the other target. Defend the recommendation against Hokie AI's strongest objection.
