Reading Guest Feedback as Words and as Meaning
Platform: Hokie AI (conversation) + local Python (run) · Submission: Thread exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Read a comment's five nearest neighbors on tf-idf and on embeddings, and say which words or which meaning tie each set to it.
  2. Explain why a comment that never says wifi or internet finds the internet complaints on embeddings and misses them on a bag of words.
  3. Explain why a comment that says a problem did not happen can sit beside comments where it did, on embeddings.
  4. Read NMF topics and BERTopic topics against a department column, count the topics that hold one department, and trace where a word with two senses went.
  5. Choose which topic list to give a hotel's operations manager for routing comments, and name what to warn the manager about.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Open text_explorer.py and open guest_feedback.csv from its window. Set Text column to comment, Topics k to 10, LSA components to 5, Keep terms in at least this many documents to 2, and Minimum topic size to 10. It opens at 15, so change it. The Embeddings box names guest_feedback_embeddings.csv with Match rows by comment_id. If it says No embeddings file, press Choose file and pick guest_feedback_embeddings.csv. Press Run the analysis. The page opens in your browser.

Check three things before you read anything. The line under the page title reads 400 documents, 1041 terms, DTM 400 x 1041 with 7799 nonzero cells (1.87 percent full). Embeddings matched for every document; 10 BERTopic topics. Your topic count at the end of that line may differ. On the Map tab, with Color by set to department, Find GF20129 and press Find, and the first neighbor in the panel on the right is GF20392 at 0.249 when Weights is tf-idf.

You read two comments, each twice. The neighbor panel lists five neighbors with their cosine, their comment_id and, in italics, their department, because Color by is set to department. The words a neighbor shares with your comment are highlighted in yellow.

GF20129 is a guest whose internet kept cutting out. Read it in the panel. With Weights on tf-idf, write down the five neighbors, their departments, how many of the five are about the internet connection, and the highlighted words each one shares with GF20129. Then set Weights to embeddings and write down the same things.

GF20216 is a happy guest at the beach property. Find it, read it, and do the same twice. Here, count how many of the five neighbors are complaints. A complaint is any neighbor whose department is not Guest relations. On the embeddings, say what each complaint is about. Then open the Matrix tab, type GF20216 in Documents, empty the Terms box, and press Show. The columns are the words GF20216 keeps in its bag of words.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one predicts before you report AND move two reads GF20129 both ways AND move three reads GF20216 both ways AND move four judges which neighbors to trust for which kind of comment. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Write in your prompt that you read a tool Hokie AI cannot see, that you will report what the tool shows, and that you want each reading tested. Quote GF20129 in full and say which words a guest usually writes for this problem, such as wifi, internet, signal and connection, and which of them GF20129 uses. Predict, before any neighbor, what a bag of words will match it with and what embeddings will match it with, and why. Ask Hokie AI to argue against your prediction.

Move two reports GF20129's five neighbors on tf-idf and on embeddings, with their departments and how many are about the internet connection. Name the highlighted words that tie the tf-idf neighbors to GF20129, and say what those words describe. Say what the embeddings matched instead.

Move three quotes GF20216 in full and reports its five neighbors on tf-idf and on embeddings, with how many are complaints. On Tuesday you saw that not, no and never are stop words, which the bag of words drops. Report the words GF20216 keeps from the Matrix tab, and say what the complaints among its embedding neighbors are about and what part of GF20216 they echo.

Move four judges. The hotel wants to route each comment to the department that should read it. Say, for a comment written in unusual words like GF20129 and for a happy comment that mentions problems that did not happen like GF20216, which method's neighbors you would trust, and why. Defend the judgment against Hokie AI's strongest objection.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 35 for the exchange in the M block and 15 for the reflection.

Case one read two comments. Case two reads all 400 at once, through the topics each method finds. Use the same page you opened in case one.

Open the Topics tab. It shows the 10 NMF topics from tf-idf, each with its top words, and below them a table headed Topics against a column. Set Column to department. Then open the BERTopic tab, which shows BERTopic's topics from the embeddings, and below them a table headed BERTopic topics against a column. Set Column to department there too.

Check two things. The Topics tab lists T1 with top words bed, bug, single, hard and amazing, and in its table T1 has 32 comments in Guest relations. The note above the BERTopic cards names the number of topics your run found, and the outlier card shows how many comments are outliers. The reference run found 10 topics and 13 outliers. Your run may find 9 to 12 topics, and the topic numbers may be in a different order, so use your own.

A topic holds one department when at least 90 percent of its comments come from that department. For each topic in each table, divide the largest count in its row by the row total. Count the topics that reach 0.90, without the outlier row. Then find three things in each table. The topics that list cold among the ten words on their card, the topics that list charge among those ten words, and the topics where the Guest relations comments went. The NMF table lists its rows in text order, T1, T10, T2 and on, so read each row by its label. To read the comments inside a topic, open the Map tab, set Color by to topic (NMF) or topic (BERTopic), double click that topic in the legend so it shows alone, and hover over its points.

Four moves, in order. The four moves are on the card below.

Move one reads the NMF table AND move two reads the BERTopic table AND move three traces cold, charge and the happy guests in both AND move four recommends a topic list to the hotel's operations manager. All four moves.

Move one opens the block. Report how many of the 10 NMF topics hold one department, and name two topics that do and two that do not, each with its top words and its row of counts. Ask Hokie AI what the mixed topics have in common.

Move two reports the same for BERTopic on your run, with your number of topics and outliers. Say which method has more topics that hold one department.

Move three traces three things in both tables. Name the topics with cold among their top words and say whether the cold room and the cold breakfast are kept apart. Do the same for charge, a billing charge against a phone that would not charge. Then find where the 66 Guest relations comments went in each method, name those topics with their top words, and say what NMF's T1 is built from, after reading its comments on the Map tab. If your BERTopic run gave the praise a topic of its own, say how many Guest relations comments it holds and where the rest went.

Move four recommends one topic list to the operations manager, who wants new comments sent to the right department, and names what to warn the manager about in the list you chose. Defend the recommendation against Hokie AI's strongest objection.
